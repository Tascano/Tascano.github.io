A guide for the reader who wants to go beyond this book. It lists works found in a catalogue search on 21 November 2026 that the main chapters do not cite. Most were identified from library catalogue records. Items marked “text read” were fetched and searched in full; all the others are leads, not endorsements. Where a work is a novel or a video, that is said.

Books on Bassein and the Portuguese north

  • Teresa Albuquerque, Bassein, the Portuguese Interlude (2004), Baçaim to Vasai (2006) and The Portuguese Impress: Glimpses of the Portuguese Possessions of Goa, Bombay and Bassein (2017). [n55; n59]

  • Braz A. Fernandes, Armas e inscrições do Forte de Baçaim (1957): on the arms and inscriptions of the fort. [n59]

  • Diogo da Costa, Relaçam das guerras da India desde o anno de 1736 até o de 1740 (1741): a Portuguese account of the wars that ended with the 1739 siege. [n59]

  • A. F. Moniz, Noticias e Documentos para a Historia de Damão, antiga Província do Norte (1900), text read in part: documents on Damão, the neighbouring Portuguese town of the northern province, with many mentions of Baçaim. [n59]

  • Francisco Lameira, Retábulos na Província do Norte (2019): on altarpieces of the northern province. [n59]

  • Amita Kanekar and Surendra Kumar, Portuguese Sea Forts (2020); Sachin Pendse, Maritime Heritage of Konkan (2011); Sudha Srivastava, The Environmental History of Maritime Forts in and around Mumbai (2008). [n59]

  • Note on the condition of the buildings in the Portuguese fort of Bassein (Curator of Ancient Monuments, Bombay, 1883): a Government survey of the fort. [n59]

  • Josef Wicki, S.J., ed., O Livro do «Pai dos Cristãos» (Lisbon, 1969), text read for Baçaim: the register of decrees for the converts of Goa and the north, including the Bassein decrees of 1547, 1549, 1550 and 1585. [n71]

  • António da Silva Rego, ed., Documentação para a História das Missões do Padroado Português do Oriente: Índia, vols 3 and 12, text read for Baçaim: Miguel Vaz’s memorial and the royal letters of 1545–46, the Piedade friars’ requests of 1547, Valignano’s Sumario of about 1579–80 with its account of Bassein, Thana and Bandra, the Jesuit annual letters of 1580–81 and the Viceroy’s safe-conduct for Bassein’s gentiles of 1578. [n72; n73]

  • Alberto Lobato, Relações Luso-Maratas, 1658–1737 (Lisbon, 1965), text read in part (chapters VI–VIII, parts of IX and X): the Portuguese archive record of the Maratha raids on Bassein’s lands, 1723–32, and the fortification of Thana. [n74]

  • P. S. S. Pissurlencar, ed., Assentos do Conselho do Estado, vol. V (1696–1750), text read for 1738–39: the Goa State Council’s minutes on Bassein, the Maratha terms and the vote of 25 March 1739 to cede Bassein. [n75]

  • P. S. S. Pissurlencar, “A Campanha Luso-Marata de Baçaim”, Boletim do Instituto Vasco da Gama 52 (1942), text read: the Portuguese-language account of the 1737–39 war, with a dated chronology from 1719 and figures on the holders of Bassein’s villages. [n76]

  • A. B. de Bragança Pereira, “Os Portugueses em Baçaim”, and R. M. Telles, “Epigrafia de Baçaim”, in O Oriente Português nos. 7–9 (Nova Goa, 1930s; archive.org dli.ernet.535714), text read in part: the 1726 parish table, the pacarias of Bassein and Agashi and the Jesuit granary. [n77]

  • Sebastião Gonçalves, S.J., Primeira Parte da História dos Religiosos da Companhia de Jesus (ed. J. Wicki), vols II and III, text read in part: Book 7, chapters 6 and 8 on the Jesuits at Baçaim and Thana. [n78]

  • Contemporary Portuguese and Spanish chroniclers, text read for Baçaim: Gaspar Correia, Lendas da Índia (tomo III, 1528–38, including the 1531 Thana raid, the council on Baçaim’s timber trade and the assault of 20 January 1533); Diogo do Couto, Da Ásia (Déc. IV, Books 8–10 on the 1533 assault, the 1534 treaty and the first captains; Déc. V on 1537–44: the Mir Zaman treaty, Garcia de Sá, the duel and the arrest of Dom Manuel de Lima, the Karnala forts; Déc. VI on the Diu relief, Castro at Baçaim, the Turkish alarms of 1552 and 1554; Déc. VII on 1554–62 (the garrison, the northern galleons, Dom Pedro de Almeida and the São Martinho), the Damão campaign of 1559 and the purchase of Asserim; Déc. VIII on the Koli wars and the siege of Chaul; Déc. IX–XII on the 1570s–90s; Déc. X on the northern provinces and Fernão de Miranda’s fleet); João de Barros, Da Ásia (Déc. IV, Books 6–8, on 1535–38 including the Mir Zaman treaty text); the Lisbon edition of 1778–88 (archive.org volumes daasiadejoodeba00lavagoog to …20lavagoog); Garcia de Orta, Colóquios dos simples (1563), with Ficalho’s notes; Manuel de Faria y Sousa, Asia Portuguesa (Spanish). [n80; n82; n83; n85; n90–n102]

  • Dutch and English travellers, text read: Linschoten’s Itinerario (1596; English 1598), the Journal of John Jourdain, the appendix to The Travels of Peter Mundy, vol. II, and Robert Orme’s Historical Fragments. [n81]

Books on the communities

  • A. N. Weling, The Katkaris (1934); A. M. Ghatage, Warli of Thana (1969); Archana Prasad, Red Flag of the Warlis (2017); Wendell D’Cruz, A Warli-Christian Story (1999). [n57; n59]
  • S. N. Gajendragadkar, East Indian Fisher Folk (1970). [n59]
  • Momin Mohiuddin, Muslim Communities in Medieval Konkan, 1610–1900 (2002). [n59]
  • Armando Menezes and D. N. Shanbhag, Essays on Konkani Language and Literature (1970). [n59]
  • First Report of the Bombay East Indian Association (1890). [n59]

Books on the wider history

  • The Portuguese in India (Cambridge, 1987), including its chapters “The Portuguese arrival in India” and “Indo-Portuguese society”. [n59]
  • Suraj Pandit, The Age of the Traikutakas (2012) and Mumbai Beyond Bombay (Marathi edition translated by Prashant Gharat). [n55; n59]
  • Alexander Kyd Nairne, History of the Konkan (1988 reprint). [n59]

Literature

  • Citra Mudgal, Post Box No. 203, Nala Sopara (Hindi, 2016; English translation 2022). A novel set in Nala Sopara, not a history. [n59]

Archives

  • British Library, India Office Records, IOR/G/38, Factory Records: Thana (1775–1799). [n59]
  • British Library, India Office Records: IOR/H/620 (papers on the Treaty of Bassein, 1802, and the Maratha War); IOR/H/576 (William Palmer, Resident at Poona, 1800–02); Mss Eur F128/203 (Strachey at Bassein, February–April 1803); IOR/G/36/102 (two Englishmen at Bassein, 1636). British Library photographs: Photo 598 (ruined Bassein churches, 1870s) and Photo 1009/5 (Henry Cousens, 1898, Bassein and Sopara). [n67]
  • Torre do Tombo, Lisbon (DigitArq catalogue). [n59]

Viewing

Several videos on Vasai Fort and one on Vasai’s culture and landmarks are on YouTube. Their makers and accuracy were not checked; see note 59 for the titles and links. No documentary on Sopara, Gass, the Kuparis or the Vadvals was found. [n59]

What was not found

No source in Chinese, and no further Dutch, French or Italian material, was found beyond what the main chapters already use. HathiTrust and Gallica could not be searched from the research environment, and Teresa Albuquerque’s three books on Bassein could not be obtained lawfully, so they are cited only from catalogue records. The 1802 Treaty of Bassein is treated in Chapter 09 from the sources in note 61. [n59; n61]
