How to use this appendix

  • Hindu dates move with the lunar calendar; months given (Kartik, Shravan, Chaitra) are the Hindu months and the Gregorian month is approximate. Christian feasts are fixed dates unless stated. Where a source gives a date for one year only, the year is shown.
  • Evidence flags: 1882 = the Gazetteer; DOC = news or official record; TRAD = blog or community account; gap = no Vasai-specific source found. Most general Maharashtra festivals (Gudi Padwa, Diwali, Holi/Shimga, Paryushan) have no Vasai-specific source in the notes and are listed under “no source found”.
  • Citations [nNN; source].

2. Detail by festival

2.1 Fairs, yatras and feasts (detail)

Nirmal yatra (November / Kartik)

  • Kartik Vadya 11 (November), 1882: week-long fair, 6,000-7,000 pilgrims (Hindus, Muslims, Christians, a few Parsis) from Thana, Gujarat, Bombay, the Deccan and the south Konkan; brass and copper vessels, dried plantains, sweets, cloth, cattle; about Rs 10,000. Da Cunha: “jatra annually held on the 11th day of Kartika”. [n11; n40; DC p.127]
  • Today (Lokmat, Agrowon 19 Nov 2025): yatra begins on Kartik (Prabodhini) Ekadashi and runs about 15 days; students’ dindi, then a palkhi with the Shankaracharya’s image; bathing in Vimal Talav with pinda-daan and tarpan; stalls from Nirmal Naka to Bhuigaon Phata selling toys, sweets, dry fruit, Vasai sukeli, dates, pedhe, singhade (water chestnuts) and groundnuts; Konkan roots konfal (purple yam), kanak and ratali (sweet potato) in demand; municipal arrangements and extra police. Blog: 14-day yatra. Timing in 2026 is a calendar inference (Ekadashi early November). [n20; n10]

Jivdani (Navratri, Dussehra)

  • Navratri crowds; Dussehra fair draws thousands; Sundays; temple dates conflict (about 150 years; 17th century; prana-pratishtha 1956; 1,500+ years); fort captured by Chimaji Appa 31 March 1739 (Marathi Wikipedia; Fall of Vasai itself 16 May 1739 per n32); Jivdani elder sister of Ekvira, Baronda Devi younger. 1882: “fair season” for barren women with betel-nut offerings. [n20; n10; n12]

3. Customs, rites and seasonal religion

3.1 Customs and rites calendar (life-cycle and seasonal), with food (notes 34, 41, 07)

3.1a Kupari and Christian

  • Birth: wife goes to her parents’ house; on the third or sixth night the spirit Sathi is guarded against by strewing gram at the door; baptism between the 8th and 15th day, godfather (padrinho) and godmother (madrinha); mother absent from the christening; poorer classes burn incense at the door and feast on country liquor, dates, gram and molasses; purification at church on the 40th day. Hindu Samvedis: Sati worshipped on the fifth day, naming on the twelfth (same spirit, evidence of continuity). [n34; Gaz. XIII pp.82, 210-211]
  • Marriage: Salsette girl’s parents propose; in Bassein the proposal comes from the boy’s side; dowry Rs 50 to 500 (up to 5,000) in ornaments; couple instructed 2-3 months before; confession and communion a day or two before; weddings 8-10 am (Kolis in the afternoon with native music); groom under a long silk umbrella and, in lower classes, in old European dress with scarlet or black coat, cocked hat and epaulets (costume hired Rs 5-10); bride in a palanquin (duli); groom gives a gold necklace worn while he lives; feast with two pork dishes with vinegar and spices; Hindu Samvedis: marry girls before ten, groom rides a horse, widow marriage forbidden. [n34; Gaz. XIII pp.82-83, 211-214]
  • Death: priest, bell tolled, burial within 24 hours, poor in a calico habit; widow gives up ornaments; brotherhoods with candles; seventh-day prayers and meal; month and year services; graves temporary Rs 2-15, permanent Rs 25-200. Hindu Samvedis cremate (except children under three), gather ashes on day 3, feed the village Mhar, mourn ten days. [n34]
  • Dress: 1882 men: wealthy in black European dress, poorer in coloured knee trousers; cart-drivers white cylindrical cotton hat or woollen night-cap; fishermen red broadcloth caps; Hindu Samvedi men also wear red broadcloth caps “like Telegu Brahmans” (so the red cap is not a convert’s mark; Swarajya’s claim unsupported). Women: Hindu fashion, dark purple, green or black; white sheet-like robe for church; gold cockle-shell earrings, silver double-loop necklaces, glass bangles, silver anklets (vales); widows give up bangles, mugdya, poth necklace. Community: men dhoti, white shirt, black sleeveless jacket, red cap, left-ear earring; women red blouse with red checked kashta sari (“lal lugda”); mostly disappeared (All About Vasai Virar 2026). East Indian lugra: hand-draped ten-yard checked cotton sari, silk with zari border; worn for the Moya (pre-wedding) and Umbra-cha Paani (fetching bathing water). [n07; n34]
  • Seasonal religion: passion plays introduced by the Jesuits mid-16th century (society of penitents 1551, brought to Bassein 1552 by Fr Melchior Nunes Barreto); first fruits of the rice harvest blessed at church; secret offerings to Ganpati and Gauri and vows to Shitladevi in outlying villages; Lenten Pirsao and Upas; Palm Sunday Ramacha Pass. [n34; DC pp.249-250]
  • Music: each church paid one or two violin music masters; Nandakhal songs (bride leaving her father, women fetching fodder, groom’s first shave, daughter returning after rice planting) “rarely heard”; Vadval south Vasai music “more fun-loving”; brass bands, ghumat (clay drum) at Christmas; Mumbai’s first East Indian Battle of the Bands. [n34; n13; n07] ### Vadval / Panchkalshi (Enthoven 1922; Gazetteers 1882, 1909)
  • Gods: Cheda and other demon-gods beside Ganpati, Shiv, Krishna (1882); Enthoven: Shaivas and Vaishnavas, household goddesses Mahalakshmi, Ekvira, Vajreshwari, Jogeshwari; worship at weddings and annual jatra; important holiday: Chaitra amavasya; Mahalakshmi of Kolvan near Dahanu; Marathi Wikipedia: kuldev Khandoba; SKP Pithori Amavasya with 64 yoginis. Weddings: no dowry; bride’s family gives nose-ring, ear-ring, “five copper pots”, ring(s); rites agnisthapana, simantpujan, madhuparka, antarpat, lajahom, saptapadi; sword or dagger piercing the canopy; eight married “ashtavargas” as witnesses; widow remarriage by pat at night under a mango tree; matchmaking from the groom’s side; women wear yellow silk or cotton saris with embroidered borders. Gotras include Bharadwaj, Bhargav, Gargya, Gautam, Harit, Jamadagni, Kashyap, Kaushik, Kaundinya, Nagrishi, Padmaksha, Shandilya, Trimbak, Vairakshya, Vasishtha, Vishwamitra (discipleship, not descent; Rane’s Bimbakhyan 1877). Dress 1909: short waistcloth, bara bandi waistcoat with 12 fastenings, Koli cap or large red turban; women’s silver belt (kargota). Funerals: cremation, bamboo and coconut-leaf bier (zavalka), 13-day rites, Narayan bali. Proverb: “Panchkalshi ani kon mhanel alshi”. [n41; Enthoven III pp.162-167; BCG I pp.239-241]
