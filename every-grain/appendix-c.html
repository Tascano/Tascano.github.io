How to use this appendix

  • Dates are the sources’ dates. For Christian sites most “founded in 15xx” dates are parish tradition, a blog or a directory; the 1882 Gazetteer gives mainly 19th-century rebuilding dates. Every conflict is shown rather than resolved; the full list is in the table at the end (and App. G §4).
  • Citations are [nNN; source]; short forms as in App. B (Gaz., DC, BG, CRIT, EI, Hull, WP).
  • Part 1 Christian sites (fort churches, parish churches and their daughters, wayside crosses, the bells in temples, Gass). Part 2 Hindu temples and tirthas. Part 3 Jain sites. Part 4 Buddhist sites. Part 5 mosques and dargahs. Part 6 Adivasi groves and Waghoba shrines. Part 7 the Catholic significance of Vasai (earliest traces, institutions, St Gonsalo Garcia, shrines and the pilgrim calendar). Then a table of date conflicts.
  • Vasai is a Diocese (created 22 May 1998), not an Archdiocese. Holy Cross is at Nirmal; the Holy Spirit church is at Nandakhal (n09’s correction of the brief).

  • Diocese wording: first Latin-rite church in the fort 1536, “Nossa Senhora da Vida” (probably the Matriz/first parish church, not the Augustinian one); Augustinian church is N. S. da Anunciada (1596). Seven churches (five orders: Franciscans, Dominicans, Augustinians, Jesuits, Hospitallers; two secular: Matriz and Misericordia) per manickpur.com and FPJ 11 Jan 2026 (blog, unverified); 1674 records: two colleges, four convents, six churches; 1905 account: Matriz, Misericordia and thirteen churches. 1.3 Wayside crosses (khursa) and cross feasts

  • Crosses stand in almost every gaothan; at the annual cross feast villagers gather to say the rosary; the tradition predates the state of Maharashtra. Named crosses: Community Cross at Cross Naka (Agashi); St Peter’s Church cross (Koliwada); a cross on the way to Vasai Court; Community Cross at Bangli Naka; Milagres Cross near Remedy church (“miraculous”); the Parvati Cross toward Vasai station; the Furtado family cross at Kirawali-Chobare. Also: Madikar Cross, Bhuigaon (steel; “local equivalent of a Gaondevi”, Marti family); Madres Talav cross (Christmas and Nativity feast gatherings). The word “khursa” itself had no source text. [n09; n11; n40; VasaiCity; Vasai Catholics blog 2015]

1.6 Gass (note 42)

  • St Gonsalo Garcia Church, Gass: 1942 (expanded/renovated 1957); parish bifurcated from Nirmal; weeklong February feast; patron Gonsalo Garcia (born Bassein c. 1556/57, crucified Nagasaki 5 Feb 1597, canonised 8 June 1862, patron of the Diocese of Vasai, feast 7 Feb in India); attacked 11 Aug 2023. Feast days at the Vasai Fort ruin (Church of the Holy Name, “Killa cha Sann”) and Erangal were held 11 Jan 2026. [n42]
  • Sonarbhat Jain temple remains: temple “said to have been dedicated to Parasnath”, Chakreshvar bull, broken female figure (perhaps Ambika). [n42; Gaz. XIV p.341]
  • Mala (Gas lake shore): foundations of a probable 11th-12th c. Brahmanic temple and carved lotus pendant. [n42]
  • Tomb of Shaikh Akbar-moi-ud-din (Muslim saint), Sonarbhat garden. [n42]
  • Bandar Masjid near Gass-Taki Pada; Muslim graves at the Bhatela pond (Sopara boundary). [n42; Shah 2020]
  • Surya image (1412 CE) and seven Jain bronzes (1458-1555 CE) from Gass, held by Jain sanghs. [n42; Shah 2020]

Part 6. Adivasi groves and Waghoba shrines

  • Waghoba (tiger/leopard god) worship among the Warli: 150 shrines recorded across Mumbai Suburban, Thane and Palghar (Nair, Patil, Surve, Andheria, Linnell, Athreya, 2021); every Warli village visited had one; festival Waghbaras on Vasu Baras (first day of Diwali), two days, shaman-led, offerings of coconut, toddy, chickens and goats; wooden idols renewed every 15-20 years. No named Vasai-Virar devrai or Waghoba shrine found; Tungar and Jivdani hills (Warli-inhabited) likeliest. Sun-and-moon carved stones near Tungareshwar match the iconography (inference). [n10]

Part 7. Catholic significance: sites, shrines and the pilgrim calendar (note 38)

7.1 Earliest Christian traces at Sopara (tradition and record)

  • Church of St Thomas the Apostle at Supara: Jordanus of Severac (c. 1321) found many Nestorian Christians and “a church dedicated to St Thomas the Apostle”; stayed fifteen days, baptised “many people”, gave Communion to about ninety Christians; recommended Supera as a base for missions to India and Ethiopia. The 1882 Gazetteer reads it as “a relic of the old Kalyan bishopric” of Cosmas Indicopleustes (6th c., bishop at Kalliana appointed from Persia). Dedication, not proof that Thomas himself came; no Thomas-at-Sopara tradition found. [n38; Gaz. XIV p.322]
  • Tomb of the Thana martyrs at Supara (1321 or 1322): four Europeans (Thomas of Tolentino, James of Padua, Peter of Siena, and Jordanus of Severac) with the Georgian lay-brother Demetrius reached Thana, received by fifteen Nestorian families; the Muslim governor put four to death (James unharmed in the fire; three beheaded; Peter cut asunder next day). Jordanus, preaching at Sopara, “taking the bodies to Sopara, buried them there”. Friar Odoric came within a year, visited the tomb and took the bones; Thomas’s skull went to Quanzhou, Khanbaliq, then the Franciscans of Tolentino (1330), later a silver bust in the cathedral. Date conflict: 8 April 1321 (Wikipedia) vs 1322 (Gazetteer, Acta Sanctorum); feast 9 April; Odoric’s removal 1323 or 1326; cult approved Pius VII 1809 and Leo XIII 1894 (beatification vs confirmation of cult stated differently); never canonised. [n38; Gaz. XIV pp.356-357; Yule, Cathay]
  • Apostolic traditions: St Bartholomew (Eusebius/Pantaenus; Perumalil and Moraes place it near Kalyan; “the majority of scholars are skeptical”); the Diocese of Vasai itself traces Christianity to Bartholomew (claim, not proof). [n38]

7.2 Portuguese-era institutions (dates as in Hull and da Cunha)

  • 1534 Franciscans first (Antonio do Porto, “Apostle of Bassein”, 10,156 converts per Catholic Encyclopedia; orphanage at Agashi 1535, 40 boys, destroyed 1540); Jesuits 1542 (foundations 1548; Xavier visited about end 1544 and twice in 1548; Fr Melchior Gonsalves first rector; second rector Francisco Barreto later Provincial 1553); Dominican convent 1548 (church 1583); Augustinians 1572 (about 1596 church); Hospitallers after 1681. Pimenta (visitor 1598-1602) founded the College of the Purification and St Cecilia at Thana. Rural churches handed to native secular priests 1570. 1634: Franciscans 30, Jesuits 15, Dominicans 10, Augustinians 8; Jesuits drew 3,000 pardaos a year. Fort: five convents, a “cathedral wrongly so called, in reality a matriz”, six other churches and an orphan asylum (Hull); Fryer 1674: six churches, four convents, two colleges. Vicar-General of the North at Bassein until 1739 (then Thana, Kurla, Gloria Church Bombay). Inquisition Commissary at Bassein (Dellon held 1673; prison slab by the travellers’ bungalow; Goa Inquisition 1560-1812); Inquisition stopped pilgrimage to a healing tank near Our Lady of Remedies (da Cunha p.163). 1588: 9,400 baptisms (unverified). Dates conflict: Hull: Papdy built 1565 (rebuilt 1864), Douli/Remedy 1583 (rebuilt 1839), Sandor 1575, Nirmal 1580 church “destroyed by the Mahrattas in 1737”. [n38; Hull I pp.1-6, 21-22; DC pp.231-236]
  • 1739 capitulation (Hull pp.75-76): liberty of Christian worship; privileges of three churches in Bassein plus one in the district and one in Salsette; images to remain. Tiefenthaler 1750: nobles and regular clergy left for Goa, a native priest remained with Maratha approval. [n38]
  • Jurisdiction: Goa 1534/35-1886 (Padroado); Diocese of Daman from 23 June 1886 (Concordat) / bull Humanae Salutis 1 Sept 1886; Bombay from 1928 (Inter Apostolicam 1 May 1928); Diocese of Vasai 22 May 1998 (Cum ad aeternam, announced 28 June 1998). 1886 Catholic Encyclopedia: Damao diocese 71,000 Catholics, 51 churches; Bassein district 12 churches and 1 chapel; Bassein varado 19,527 Catholics (Goa Annuario 1901). Bombay East Indian Association 1887. [n38]

7.3 St Gonsalo Garcia (first Indian saint)

  • Born Bassein c. 1556/57 (ANTT record: “natural de Agacaim”, i.e. Agashi; Portuguese father, Canarese/Indian mother); Jesuit school at the fort (1564-1572); to Japan at 15 (1572) or c. 1580; catechist, merchant (Macao), Franciscan lay brother in Manila, interpreter 1592; arrested December 1596; ears cut 3 Jan 1597; crucified Nagasaki 5 Feb 1597 with 25 others; Urban VIII bull 14 Sept 1627 (blessed per da Cunha; “venerable” per Catholic Encyclopedia); canonised by Pius IX 8 June 1862; feast 6 Feb, moved to 7 Feb in India; patron of the Diocese of Vasai, second patron of the Archdiocese of Bombay. Cult lapsed from about 1739 and revived. [n38]
  • Sites and feast: Church of the Holy Name (ruined Jesuit church), Vasai Fort: feast and fair “Killa Cha Sann” on the first Sunday nearest the neap tide after Christmas (11 Jan 2026; DNA 2014 “thousands”; families arrive the night before and cook East Indian dishes; once by cart or boat); St Gonsalo Garcia Church, Gass (1942, “major shrine”, not “national shrine”: unconfirmed; one n38 source says built by Msgr Louis Caetan D’Souza); also Erangal feast (St Bonaventure). John Paul II named him at Vasai on 9 Feb 1986 (speech at St Augustine’s High School, Vasai, first stop of his Bombay visit). St Gonsalo Garcia College, Vasai (1984; 750-bed COVID-19 centre 2020). Relics: none found. [n38; FPJ 2026] ### 7.4 Other shrines and recent pilgrim sites
  • Shrine of Our Lady of Remedies, Remedy (1577 GCatholic vs 1583 Hull “Douli”; minor seminary; Bishop Thomas D’Souza rector 2013-2019; Milagres Cross). Holy Cross, Nirmal (feast 14 September; 2012 Way of the Cross walked about fourteen old churches). Jubilee 2025: opened 29 Dec 2024 at the Cathedral, Papdy; Mini Jubilee Crosses blessed 4 March 2025. St Joseph’s, Umralla (Umrale): designated Pilgrim Centre for the Year of St Francis (Pope Leo XIV, 10 Jan 2026 to 10 Jan 2027; 800th anniversary of Francis’s death) on 7 June 2026 with plenary indulgence. [n38]
