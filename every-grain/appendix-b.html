How to use this gazetteer

  • Each entry gives old and Portuguese names, first mention, communities, parish, census figures, landmarks, a short history and a pointer to the chapters (Ch. numbers follow the plan: 04 Port of the Western Sea; 05 Shilahara kings; 06-08 Sultans and Portuguese; 09 Maratha Vasai; 10-12 Company and freedom; 13-15 Republic to 2026; K1-K7 the Kuparis; T1-T3 threads).
  • Citations are [nNN; source]. Short forms: Gaz. = Bombay Gazetteer vol. XIV, Thana: Places of Interest (1882) (XIII = vol. XIII Part I); DC = da Cunha, Chaul and Bassein (1876); BG = the Gazetteer’s Bassein entry; CRIT = the 2008 MMR heritage survey; EI = east-indians.net parish pages; Hull = Bombay Mission History; Tombo = Tombo de Baçaim 1727-30; WP = Wikipedia.
  • Spelling: Gass (never “Gas”) in the book’s own voice; sources’ spellings are quoted as written. Diocese of Vasai, not Archdiocese.
  • Census 2011 figures exist only for the 50 villages outside the Vasai-Virar City Municipal Corporation (VVCMC); every other place was inside the corporation, so it has no village figure (see the VVCMC entry and App. G). Most “founded in 15xx” church dates are parish tradition; the Gazetteer gives the 19th-century rebuilding dates; where sources conflict both are shown.
  • Kuparis: every entry ends with a “Kuparis:” line saying whether Kuparis are recorded there, with their parish, conversion-era and 1882 history, Tombo landholders, families, customs, people and present life as far as the notes allow (notes 07, 09, 34, 38, 39, 40, 42, 44, 46, 49), or “No Kupari presence recorded in the notes”.
  • Corrections carried forward: Nala did exist in 1882 (n40 corrects n31); the Gazetteer’s Agashi temple date 1691 and “1580” ship-building date are suspect (see Agashi); Rock Edict IX was found at Bhuigaon in 1956 (n02), not Mardes; Grierson’s “Alabim” is probably Mahim.

Achole

  • 1882 itinerary sent visitors “a mile and a half to the north of Manikpur” to see “its Portuguese fort”; the Tungar road “passes between the villages of Achola and Rajavli to Gokirva.” About half a mile south of Bab Naik’s house at Sopara, on the way to Achola, ran the old raised basalt footway (“stone dam”, dagdi bandh). Today Achole is in Nallasopara East. [n12; Gaz. 1882]

  • Kuparis: No Kupari presence recorded in the notes. Achole is on the 1882 list of Bassein villages with Urap Agris, Christian Agris who returned to Hinduism in 1820 or 1828, a different community from the Kuparis (GzXIII-1 p.117 n.1). [n40; n49]

Agashi

  • Old names: Portuguese “Agazim”; the island’s northern garden belt is the agar of Agashi. [n11; Gaz. 1882 p.1]
  • Place: town and port about 10 miles north of Bassein, joined to Virar station by a metalled road; the bay mouth closed by a reef except for one channel. [n11; Gaz. 1882 p.1]
  • First mention / history: “probably an old timber trade and ship-building centre”. c. 1530 it built ships “as good as Portuguese ships, able to make the voyage to Europe” (the Gazetteer prints “1580”, probably a slip for 1530). The town and 300 boats were burnt by the Portuguese; Faria y Souza notes a second destruction in March 1531. 1535: Franciscan Antonio do Porto founded the orphanage of Nossa Senhora da Luz for 40 boys; 1540: a retreating Gujarat force burnt it (by Portuguese accounts with the orphans inside). 1554: a great mosque became the commandant’s residence. 1570: “Agazim” a port trading with Gujarat. 1618 hurricane blew a boat into a house. 1634: Mughal raiders from Gujarat destroyed a “handsome Dominican monastery”. Customs and revenue 1536: 281,249 fedeas; 1537: 313,874. 1760: Anquetil du Perron found lanes full of people going to church. 1781: British prepared to bombard Arnala from Agashi. [n11; Gaz. 1882 pp.1-2, 11]
  • Communities and economy: 1837 “chief town on Bassein island”; Christian and Hindu; trade in grain, garden produce and salt; boat-building. 1881 population 6,823 (5,168 Hindus, 1,500 Christians, 155 Muslims); dried plantains “the best in the district”; sea exports averaged Rs 3,18,470 and imports Rs 85,650 (five years to 1879). [n11; Gaz. 1882 p.2]
  • Parish / church: church built 1568 (community blog). [n11; D’Mello blog]
  • Landmarks: Bhavani Shankar temple and lake, built 1739-1750 by Shankaraji Keshav Phadke (Chimaji Appa’s commander), said to stand on a Bimb-era fort site named in the Mahikavati Bakhar; a blog dates the Shiva and Hanuman temples c. 1691 (conflict). Hero stone and samadhi of Shankaraji Phadke were cleared by Dr Shridutt Raut’s volunteers. [n11; Durg Bharari; Heritage Chronicles]
  • See: Ch. 06-09 (shipbuilding, Portuguese raids), Ch. 11 (plantains), T1, T3; App. C (Bhavani Shankar temple); App. G.

Further detail (n40):

  • Name: no etymology in Gazetteer, da Cunha or Tombo; forms Agacaim (Tombo), “Agazim” (1570), Agasi/Aganasi (da Cunha). First dated mention: a stone found at Agashi (Collector’s garden, Thana), Shak 1072 (AD 1150), Shilahara king Haripaldev, grants the village Vatteraka (Vatar) in Shurparaka (Gaz. XIV p.385 n.1); so the oldest dated mention names Vatar, not Agashi. 1540 martyrdom (da Cunha pp.159-160): five who stayed in the orphanage refused conversion to Islam and were burnt, April 1540 under the Gujarat general “Bramaluc”, repelled at Bassein by Captain Lourenco de Tavora; “Agasi was the first place of this district visited by Franciscan missionaries”; 1634 “a rich monastery of the Dominicans”. 16 Dec 1760: Anquetil du Perron arrived on the church feast day through “the finest orchards in the world”. 1881 Christians 1,500 of 6,823; dried plantains about 160 tons a year (Rs 27,000); about 85 Samvedi Brahman, 15 Pachkalshi and 75 Native Christian families dried plantains at Agashi, Vatar and Koprad (GzXIII-1 pp.292, 395). Church of N. S. da Luz destroyed; the church after it 75 x 22 x 16 ft; vicar Rs 14-8 a month; school of 17 pupils with a violin-playing master. St James dated 1568 (CRIT: first outside the fort; “rebuilt 1760” after burning in 1739; extended 1975); EI: two churches c. 1568 (Our Lady of Light, Franciscan; St James, secular), both destroyed 1739; Fr Jovito V. Dias extended St James 1900; “Holy Martyrs” English school 1938; St James Marathi School 1968; St James High School 1977; seven villages in the parish (Mangala, Jyoti, Kumbar Wada, Bhandar Wada, Cross Naka, Dhobi Wada, Shirlai); splits: Jyoti (Our Lady of Light) and Shirlai (Our Lady of Lourdes, 1987). Gazetteer dates Bhavani Shankar temple 1691 (Shak 1613) by Shankarji Keshav Phadke (grant Rs 50), Hanuman (Rs 8), Jain temple of Parasnath built “about sixty years ago by Motilal a Vani of Bombay”; conflicts with Shankarji’s documented 1737-50 career (CRIT: “18th century, patron Peshwa”). Tombo: pacaria “Agacaim” and coloria (fl.41-42), rent on wild palms (fl.44v). CRIT precinct: Agashe Talav, Hanuman, Vishnu and Ram mandirs, Khanolkar House (Peshwa guest house), Phadkewada (“lost to high-rise construction”), Sarvajanik Vachnalaya library (est. 1914, building 1939, Seth Shah Prabhudas Bhagvandas), Zilla Parishad School (1886); Ram Mandir of the Garde family (1885) (blog). Shipbuilding declined after 1684 as the port silted (CRIT). Gram panchayat until 2009. [n40; Gaz.; CRIT; EI]

  • Kuparis: Kupari village (community lists 2007, 2009) and the start of the Kupari Christian story: “the first place of this district visited by Franciscan missionaries”; orphanage of Our Lady of Light 1535; five native converts burnt in April 1540 for refusing Islam (da Cunha pp.159-160); St Gonsalo Garcia called “natural de Agaçaim” in Lisbon (ANTT) records (tradition prefers Bassein fort). Churches c.1568; destroyed 1739; St James rebuilt c.1760; Anquetil du Perron at the feast, 16 Dec 1760. Tombo landholder D. Domingas Pereira de Azevedo (pacaria, coloria, wild-palm rent). Christians: 900 in the parish (1876), 1,500 of 6,823 in the village (1881, about 22%); about 75 Christian plantain-drier families “originally Samvedis and Pachkalshis” at Agashi, Vatar and Koprad in 1882, “gardeners who grow the fruit” (GzXIII-1 p.395); church school of 17 pupils with a violin master. Agashi is also on the 1882 Urap Agri list (Christian Agris who left the Church in 1820/1828, not Kuparis). Parish today: St James with seven wards; daughters Jyoti and Shirlai; schools 1938 (Holy Martyrs), 1968, 1977; Community Cross at Cross Naka; Yuvajallosh silver jubilee 2026 (“SAJ family”). People: Fr Ismail Da Costa (assistant priest, founded the Arnala chapel 1919). No Kupari surnames or post-1881 count recorded. See Ch. V5 IV, K2, K6. [n07; n34; n38; n39; n40; n46; n49]

Anjur

  • In Bhiwandi taluka; the Naik family of Anjur received the title Sarpatil and inam from the Peshwa (1739) for help at the siege of Bassein (GzXIII-1 p.130 n.1). Some Tombo “pragana de Anjor” aldeias (25 titles) belong to the Anjor pragana of the Thana province. [n40]

  • Kuparis: No Kupari presence recorded in the notes. Anjur is a Bhiwandi village tied to the Hindu Pachkalshi (Vadval) Naik family. [n40]

Arnala

  • Old names: Arnala Fort “also called Janjira or The Island”; Portuguese “Ilha de Vaccas” (Cows’ Island). [n11; Gaz. 1882 p.10]

  • Place: island fort guarding the southern main entrance of the Vaitarna, “the most extensive inlet in the north Konkan”. [n11; Gaz. 1882 p.10]

  • History: c. 1530 Portuguese found a strong Gujarat Sultanate fort “with domes and Saracenic arches”, destroyed it and gave the place to a Portuguese gentleman of Bassein; Wikipedia attributes original construction to Mahmud Begada (1516). Taken by the Marathas: the Marathi inscription says rebuilt under Bajirao I in 1737 (Shak 1659) by the architect Baji Tulaji; the second assault under Chimaji Appa succeeded 28 Mar 1737 (first assault with Govindji Kasar and Manaji Angre’s navy failed). British capture 18 Jan 1781 (commandant had refused to yield until bombardment was prepared); returned to Marathas at Salbai; British again 1817-18. In 1818 it ranked next to Bassein among north Konkan coast forts. A Martello-type tower stands about 550 m south. National Protected Monument since 26 May 1909 (N-MH-M13). [n11; Gaz. 1882 pp.10-11; WP Arnala Fort]

  • Landmarks (1882): Trimbakeshvar (grant Rs 45), Bhavani (Rs 91), tomb of two Muslim saints Shahali and Hajiali (Rs 80), eight-cornered stone pond. Today: Ambakeshwar, Bhavani and Shiva temples, dargahs of Shah Ali and Hajji Ali, octagonal reservoir. [n11]

  • Census 2011: Arnala Killa (island) 467 houses, 2,566 people, ST 0, SC 0, literacy 80.64%. Arnala (mainland) 3,005 houses, 15,331 people, ST 1,473, SC 117, literacy 80.10%, 7,639 workers. [n11; census2011.co.in]

  • Life today: island has no jetty (visitors wade ashore); 2015 complaint about lack of a passenger jetty; Arnala Fishermen’s Association helps in sea rescues. Flowers: Moreshwar Bhoir introduced sonchafa in the late 1980s; Arnala Farmers’ Cooperative Society has 1,100 members (chair Kiran Patil). A hut chapel was built near Arnala beach in 1919 by Fr Ismail Da Costa (weak source). [n11]

  • See: Ch. 06-09, 14; App. C; App. G.

  • Kuparis: No Kupari presence recorded in the notes. Arnala’s Catholics are Koli Christians (St Peter’s, 1919-1931). Kupari link: the 1919 chapel was founded by Fr Ismail Da Costa, assistant priest of the Kupari parish of Agashi; and the Tombo coloria of Arnala island (“Ilha das Vacas”) was held with Agaçaim by D. Domingas Pereira de Azevedo (fl.41). [n40]

Bapane, Chandip, Mandvi, Pelhar, Shirsad

  • Bapane and Chandip: nothing sourced. Pelhar and Mandvi “were once places of importance” along the Tansa valley (info from W. W. Loch); the Tansa valley land route between Vaitarna and Ulhas is one of the Gazetteer’s reasons Sopara became a trade centre (single 1882 footnote). Pelhar dam (old scheme 14 MLD) and Pelhar river pollution action plan (MPCB 2019). Shirsad: one OCR hit unchecked. [n12; Gaz. 1882; Surya EC summary 2014]

  • Kuparis: No Kupari presence recorded in the notes. [n12]

Bassein Road / Vasai Road (station)

  • Part of the BB&CI line; began operating 28 Nov 1864 (first Grant Road-Ahmedabad service), originally “Bassein Road” (code BSR); first local Grant Road-Bassein Road 1 Nov 1865 (contested “first suburban” claim); Virar’s first service 12 April 1867. Called “Manikpur” in the 1880s (see Manickpur). [n32; n31; WP, snippet]

  • Kuparis: No Kupari presence recorded in the notes. for the station itself; the 1882 Gazetteer says English-speaking Christians of Thana travelled to Bombay as clerks on the morning trains, a Salsette-centred remark with no Kupari village named. [n34]

Bhatane

  • “a landing station to the north of Tungar” with “a reservoir of dressed stones and quantities of old bricks” (W. W. Loch). 2011 population 4,786. [n12; Gaz. 1882; census2011.co.in]

  • Kuparis: No Kupari presence recorded in the notes. [n12]

Bhuigaon

  • Communities: Kupari (Samvedi Christian); the Martis extended family act as “territorial protectors”; dialect Kadodi/Kupari. A family tradition of ancestry from Orissa entertainers is unverified. [n11; Mumbai Paused blog 2011; D’Mello blog]
  • Parish: Holy Family Church founded 1946 for families who had walked far to Nirmal parish. A bishop of Vasai was born here. [n11; prayersandpetitions.org; GCatholic]
  • Landmarks / life: stainless-steel Madikar Cross in the western quarter (“the local equivalent of a Gaondevi”); crosses at front doors; about 40 large irrigation wells (bhowkal) maintained by women, growing vegetables, hybrid bananas and flowers. Blog notes urban remains buried under Nirmal’s fields. [n11]
  • See: Ch. K4-K6; App. C, E.

Further detail (n40):

  • First printed mention: da Cunha p.159 “Bhoegaum”, where the sea enters and runs through Vagholi to Vatari. Two gram panchayats, Bhuigaon Khurd and Budruk; talavs Kumbhar, Khurd, Bhola, Gorlai, Dongri; “Church - Champavati Mandir Road”. Second village after Gass to split from Holy Cross, Nirmal: Fr (later Mgr) Raymond Mendez began catechism; church 1946 on land given by poor villagers with money from the Archbishop of Bombay and Bandra parishes; independent 2 June 1946, first PP Fr Acuico Dourado; completed 1984 by Fr Nazareth Garbu; “out of the thousand Catholics only a handful attended services at Nirmal”. Thomas Dabre born 23 Oct 1945, ordained 31 Oct 1971, auxiliary bishop of Bombay 1990, first Bishop of Vasai 22 May 1998 (installed 15 Aug 1998), Bishop of Poona 2009-2023. [n40; EI; GCatholic]

  • Kuparis: Kupari village (community lists). Parish Holy Family, split from Holy Cross, Nirmal, on 2 June 1946 because “out of the thousand Catholics only a handful attended services at Nirmal”; catechism begun by Fr (later Mgr) Raymond Mendez; church on land given by poor villagers; first PP Fr Acuico Dourado. Customs: the Madikar steel cross as a Gaondevi-like protector of the Marti extended family; crosses on front doors; about 40 bhowkal wells kept by women for bananas, vegetables and flowers; correspondence and community magazine in Marathi; Kupari Kadodi said to differ from Vadval speech (Mumbai Paused 2011). Notable: Bishop Thomas Dabre, born here 23 Oct 1945, first Bishop of Vasai 1998 (no source calls him Kupari; inference from birthplace). See Ch. K6. [n07; n34; n39; n40]

Bolinj

  • Role: northern limit for boats (about 2 miles from Sopara’s old landing “Dedar”); landing for molasses, rice and betel leaf to Surat; in the north of the Sopara agar; the Bolinj creek formed the northern part of the backwater. [n11; Gaz. 1882 pp.314-316]
  • History: “formerly a large town and probably had rich temples.” A Devanagari inscribed stone served as a doorstep in the house of the Christian “Juju Manu Lop, that is Joseph Manuel Lopez”; carved stones said to lie in Devhara pond. Hamlet Koprad, half a mile south-west: a 15th-century inscribed stone near the house of the headman Bhiku Patil, a Samvedi Brahman. [n11; Gaz. 1882 p.342]
  • Communities: Kupari village. [n11; D’Mello blog]
  • See: Ch. 05, 06, 11; App. A Additions.

Further detail (n40):

  • Tombo: pacaria “Bolinja e terra Belvar”, D. Maria Henriques Coelho da Silva, 450 pardaus (fl.54v). 1882 inscription stone 2’2” long. “Between Bolinj and Sopara the road is a narrow low lane passable for wheels only in fair weather.” A 1728 Portuguese report wanted the creek dug “as deep as the height of a man”; teak rafts once floated to Sopara landing, but “for many years boats have been unable to pass beyond Bolinj”. Parish Christ the King: new centre 10 May 1988; separate parish 10 June 2001 (directory: church 2000; conflict). CRIT: Bolinj Talav (17th c.), Manukarnika Devi Talav and temple, Karmale Soneri Talav with a small dargah, Zilla Parishad school (1879). [n40]

  • Kuparis: Kupari village (community lists); plantain-drying village 1882 (GzXIII-1 p.292). Earliest named Christian of the Virar side: “Juju Manu Lop, that is Joseph Manuel Lopez”, at whose door lay the Devanagari stone (1882), a Portuguese baptismal name reshaped by Marathi speech. Tombo lessee D. Maria Henriques Coelho da Silva (450 pardaus). Catholics belonged to Nandakhal until the Bolinj centre (10 May 1988) and Christ the King parish (10 June 2001; church 2000 in a directory). The “Joseph of Bolinj” who helped start Virar’s Syro-Malabar church in 1989 has no community stated. No Kupari count or customs specific to Bolinj recorded. See Ch. V5, K6. [n39; n40; n49]

Chandansar, Manvelpada, Phoolpada

  • Not in the 1882 Gazetteer under these spellings; no sourced history (need Lokmat/Loksatta). [n12]

  • Kuparis: No Kupari presence recorded in the notes. The Tombo aldeia “Chandory/Sandory” (Manuel Pereira de Castro, fl.111v) may be Chandansar (n40 inference), a Portuguese lease but no Christian community recorded. All three lie east of the railway, where no Kupari village is recorded. [n40; n49]

Chinchoti and Tivri

  • Monsoon waterfall in the Tungareshwar foothills, popular and deadly: three deaths in 24 hours (Mid-day, July 2023); two Goregaon students drowned July 2025; 2018 one death and 100+ stranded; falls periodically closed. The Chinchoti-Anjur Phata road is the approach to Kaman. Do not confuse with Tarapur-Chinchani (Dahanu). Tivri: 1,597 people (2011). [n12; Mid-day; Lokmat Times; Tribune; Gaz. 1882]

  • Kuparis: No Kupari presence recorded in the notes. [n12]

Chulne

  • On the 1882 return route from Sopara’s stone dam, about 2 miles west of Manikpur station; listed as the birthplace of a bishop of the diocese. [n11; Gaz. 1882 p.341; GCatholic]

Further detail (n40):

  • Vadval evidence: GzXIII-1 p.97 “Christian cartmen chiefly of Chulna, Manikpur, Gokhivra, Sandor and Barhanpur in Bassein, are known as Vadvals or gardeners”; Vadvals “the best axemen in the district; their carts are larger and their cattle stronger”; bought fallen timber from Government in Vada, cut green wood in Jawhar; gangs of 5 to 30 carts; at least 12 annas a day for cart, man, boy and cattle. Tombo “Cholna Roba”: Jesuit Procurador Geral, 130 pardaus (fl.49). Practically all Catholics; Sunday Mass from 9 Sept 1951 (Fr Philip Tavares); foundation stone 22 April 1961; Our Lady of Fatima parish established 1 June 1963; church blessed 17 May 1964; talavs Chulne, Khargoda, Nazareth. Bishop Thomas Francis D’Souza born 23 March 1970 at Chulne, ordained 1998, Bishop of Vasai from 9 Nov 2024 (consecrated 15 Dec 2024). [n40]

  • Kuparis: No Kupari presence recorded in the notes. Chulne is a Christian Vadval village (cartmen, 1882), not Kupari. [n40]

Dhauli

  • Kuparis: No Kupari presence recorded in the notes. Dhauli (Davly) belongs to the Remedy parish of the southern, Vadval and Koli, Christian belt. [n32; n40]

Diwanman

  • Kuparis: No Kupari presence recorded in the notes. [n11]

Dongarpada, Holi and other villages without sources

  • No reliable village-level sources in n11 (see n12 for Gokhivare, Juchandra). Holi is a Kupari-area village name with no source. [n11]

Evershine, Navghar-Manikpur, Sativali, Waliv, Vasai Phata

  • Navghar-Manikpur was one of the four councils merged into VVCMC in 2009. Sativali (Satavli): path rising “through the Varli hamlet of Dhondvira to the temple of Shri-Tungareshvar”, about 5 miles from the hill foot (Gaz. 1882 p.367; a separate “Sativli” in Mahim is a different place). Sativali/Waliv/Gokhivare today carry small industrial estates (Tara, Samruddhi, Pride, Pinnacle, Pushpraj, Anmol; Nova RERA 24 Jan 2023). An 1881 inscribed stone (4’10” x 1’7”) was found at Manikpur. No sourced history for Evershine City, Waliv’s industrial turn, or Vasai Phata (NH-48 junction). [n12]

  • Kuparis: No Kupari presence recorded in the notes. [n12]

Gass

  • Spelling: the Gazetteer 1882 writes “Gas”; other sources “Gaas” (Shah 2020; HT 2025), “Ghas” (Wikipedia on Bishop Almeida), “Gass” (Diocese of Poona, church directories). The book uses Gass. Portuguese name: not found; no Tombo de Bacaim entry verified (the Tombo’s “Gris” is Giriz, not Gass). No standalone history book or parish chronicle exists. [n42]

  • Land: raised garden land (agar) on the south edge of old Sopara’s island, beside a landing on the silting Sopara creek: “Bolinj, Koprad and Umbrala in the north, Nirmal and Vagholi in the west, Gas in the south, and Sopara and Mardesh in the centre” (Gaz. XIV p.314). The creek “was once a considerable arm of the sea”; silting “especially since the long railway embankment has dammed the flow of water from the south”; boats of 10-12 tons (30-40 khandis) reached Gas, about a mile from Sopara; cargoes of molasses, rice and betel-leaf to Surat, Broach, Jambusar, Dholera, Bhavnagar and Gogha. 1882 one-day circuit: Manikpur, Brahma hill (about 4 miles NW), Nirmal, the “Burud-king’s tower” (stupa mound), Sopara (half a mile east), a mile south to Gas, about three miles south-east to Manikpur: about twelve miles. Vatar

  • “Vattaraka is the modern Vatargaon, two miles north-west of Sopara”: a Shilahara-era land-grant name; Anquetil du Perron (1760) noted “Outar”. [n11; Gaz. 1882 p.341 fn.]

Further detail (n40):

  • First mention: Shak 1072 (AD 1150) grant; witnesses “Risi Mhatara, headman of Vatteraka village, Naguji Mhatara, Ananta Nayaka, and Changdev Mhatara” (Gaz. XIV p.385 n.1). Tombo “Vatar Rajoly”: 700 pardaus, highest rent. Our Lady of Nazareth community centre under Nandakhal; hamlets Jeladi (108 families, 401 persons), Barodi (53 families, 187), Adlay (37 families); Don Talav; Bhongale Talav. [n40]

  • Kuparis: Christian families of Samvedi and Pachkalshi origin since at least 1882 (one of the three plantain-drier villages with Agashi and Koprad); a Kupari parish centre in a mirrored Wikipedia table, though not in the community’s thirteen-village list. Tombo “Vatar Rajoly”: Manuel Varjão da Fonseca, 700 pardaus. Catholics of Nandakhal parish through Our Lady of Nazareth centre: Jeladi 108 families/401 persons, Vatar-Barodi 53/187, Adlay 37 families (2016); Jeladi prayer centre 1990, centre 2006. Notable: Fr Francis D’Britto of Jeladi (1943-2024), Subodh Bible (Sahitya Akademi 2014), president of the 93rd Marathi Sahitya Sammelan, Harit Vasai leader. [n07; n38; n40]

Virar and Jivdani hill (Jivdhan)

  • Virar 1882: about 7 miles north of Bassein, 38 miles north of Bombay; station with refreshment and waiting rooms; passengers 48,294 (1878) to 83,176 (1880); goods 1,730 to 3,349 tons; metalled road 4 miles west to Agashi; “a rich village well-wooded and with many patches of sugarcane.” Virar East market: Saturday market on the west bank of a large pond under banyan and mango trees, “thronged by Kolis and Varlis, selling fish, garden stuff, and cloth.” Portuguese traces: church and well 50 yards south of the station; tower or fortified mansion on a knoll a mile south; a carved stone with female figures on Virar lake’s west bank; a land-grant stone with cow and calf 100 yards east of the station; ruins of a Portuguese fort on a rocky hill further east. Rail freight: molasses, rice, betel leaves north to Ankleshvar, Nadiad, Viramgam, Patan; plantains and betel leaves south to Bombay; oilcake imported as manure. [n12; Gaz. 1882 p.382]
  • Cow-footprint legend: two cow’s feet cut in rock about 200 yards beyond the cow-and-calf stone: the Mhar herdsman followed an unpaid cow to Jivdhan’s top; the goddess vanished at “Do not touch me, I am a Mhar”; the cow leapt to the rock. [n12; Gaz. 1882 pp.382-383]
  • Jivdhan hill 1882: a mile east of the station; fortification ruins, “very old-looking caves and cisterns said to be the work of the Pandavs”; barren women make offerings to an invisible deity; betel nuts thrown into a niche hole. One of two inland forts of the Bassein sub-division with Kamandurg. Today: hilltop temple often called about 150 years old; step counts 1,375 to over 1,500 conflict; ropeway/funicular planned (Jan 2021). Folk etymology Virar < Eka-Veera unconfirmed. [n12; Gaz.; WP Jivdani Mata; Onmanorama 2020]
  • See: Ch. 09-11, 14; App. C (Jivdani).

Further detail (n40):

Modern Virar West, however, includes the Kupari villages and parishes of Nandakhal, Bolinj, Shirlai, Agashi, Umrale, Nanbhat, Vatar and Rajodi; the Catholic centres of Virar East and Virar West were cut from Nandakhal (undated), and the make-up of their congregations is not recorded. Tombo “Virar Naringuy” (D. Pedro de Meneses) was a Crown lease with unnamed tenants. See Ch. V5 IV. [n39; n40; n46; n49]

Coast, creeks, islands (cross-reference for Vasai taluka)

  • Island of Sopara/Bassein and creeks: garden strip about 3 miles broad between the Vasai creek and the Vaitarna; Manikpur creek (south), Sopara creek (centre), Bolinj creek (north); “once a considerable arm of the sea”. Nirmal lake: “a fine view across the great Nirmal lake to the sea” from the Shankaracharya temple; stone steps down the west side. Forts (1882): sea forts Arnala, Bassein, Kharlio; inland forts Jivdhan and Kamandurg; Tandulvadi one of six inland forts of Mahim. Jivdhan near Nana’s Thumb in Shahapur is a different place. Beaches: Vasai Beach at the Ulhas mouth (“Mini Goa”), Suruchi Beach in Vasai Nagar, Rajodi between Kalamb and Arnala (weak). Tungareshwar sanctuary: about 600 plant species, 250+ bird species, 150 butterflies, 36 herpetofauna; 2019 Supreme Court order led to demolition of an illegal ashram structure; temple about 3 km from the base. Kamandurg: four streams to cross, five rock cisterns (state tourism page, weak); Chinchoti waterfall about 14 km from Vasai station. Tandulwadi: 1454 Sultan of Ahmedabad took Mahikavati and made Mallik Allauddin chief (Wikiwand) vs c. 1429 under Jafar Khan (n13): conflict. [n39]
