Book Three

The People and the Threads

Who carried the story, and what ran through all of it: the Kuparis of the Catholic villages, and five threads that cross every period.

The first two books say what happened and where. This one says who, and how it held together.

It begins with the Kuparis, the Catholic community whose villages lie along the old Portuguese parishes. Their story runs from the Samaveda Brahmins of the eleventh century through the mass baptisms of the sixteenth and the return of the Marathas to the present, and it explains why the Catholic villages of this coast are what they are. Then five threads are followed through all the centuries together: the sea and its traders; how each community came and how its language was formed; how each settlement was born; the Catholic story; and what people ate. The last chapter is a guide for anyone who wants to go and see what survives.

Part Eleven — The Kuparis

The Kuparis are the Catholic community whose villages lie round Vasai. Their story is the one that ties the whole book together, because it passes through nearly every period: Brahmin migrants from the Samaveda tradition, the Portuguese baptisms, the Maratha restoration and the modern parish. These six chapters tell it from the first written trace of the community to the village feast of today.
