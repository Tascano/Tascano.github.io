Period covered: 2010 – November 2026

In this chapter. The corporation takes its seat, a district is carved, and the megacity arrives. This chapter brings the story to November 2026.

In the last week of July 2024, about three thousand people came to the Sacred Heart Church at Nandakhal for a funeral. The man in the coffin was Fr Francis D’Britto, Franciscan, Marathi writer, translator of the Bible, and convenor of the movement that had once filled the Vasai grounds with a hundred thousand people to save the green belt. He had died on 25 July, aged eighty-one by one count. One of the advocates who had worked beside him in the movement, Noel Dabre, told a reporter: “The greenery that you see now is because of the movement.” [n29; FPJ obituary, 25 Jul 2024]

The greenery he meant was still there. From the church at Nandakhal, in the old garden belt of Sopara, the coconut and banana groves still ran west towards the sea, and the bavkhal ponds, those that survived, still held the monsoon water. But two kilometres to the east, along the railway, rose the other city, the one the movement had not been able to stop: a corporation of well over a million people, with towers on reserved land, water that came in tankers, floods every monsoon, and a politics dominated for three decades by one family. Four months after the funeral, that family would lose every assembly seat it held. Within thirteen months a building would fall down in Virar in the middle of the night and kill seventeen people, and the former head of the municipal corporation would be arrested by federal investigators. Within eighteen months the family’s party would win back the corporation, with a smaller majority, and a Catholic councillor would become deputy mayor.

This last chapter of the Republic follows the coast from the seating of the first mayor in March 2010 to the research cut-off in November 2026. It is the most heavily documented period in the whole book, and the least digested. Much of it rests on newspaper reports, many of them seen only in search summaries, because the major papers’ pages could not be opened. Where the sources conflict, both versions are given. Where people are accused of crimes, the accusations are reported as accusations, and where a court has ruled, its ruling is stated. Where a story has not ended, the chapter says so.

2010–2012: the corporation takes its seat

The new Vasai-Virar City Municipal Corporation, constituted on 3 July 2009 out of four municipal councils, Vasai, Virar, Nallasopara and Navghar-Manikpur, and fifty-three villages, seated its first mayor on 28 March 2010. His name was Rajeev Patil, and he served until 4 January 2012. [n26; Wikipedia: VVCMC] The first deputy mayor was Sagir Ahmed Dange. [n26] The parties of most of the early mayors are not stated in the source table, but the corporation was the Bahujan Vikas Aaghadi’s from the start; the research notes describe “a run of BVA-linked mayors from 2010”. [n26, §3] Wikipedia says elsewhere that Pravina Thakur, Hitendra Thakur’s wife, was the first mayor; the mayors’ table on the same page gives her term as 2015 to 2017, and the notes treat the “first” claim as unsourced. [n29, §2 Gaps; n26]

The corporation’s very date of birth is disputed in the documents of these years. Environmental-clearance papers written in 2014 and 2017 for a new water project describe “VVMC” as established in 2010. [n29; Surya project summary, 2014] The corporation’s own site says 3 July 2009. The notes suggest the 2010 date reflects the first elected body, not the creation. [n29, §5]

Its boundaries were disputed too. A local blog of September 2011 reported that the state had issued a notification excluding twenty-nine of the fifty-three villages, and that a court case had followed. [n29; vasairoad blog, Sept 2011] The outcome of that case was not found in any source the research could read. [n29, §5 Gaps] What is certain is that the villages had not wanted to come in, and that the question of which green villages belonged inside the city, and on what terms, remained open.

The census of 2011

The census taken in 2011, the last census of India held before this book was written, gave the first full portrait of the new city.

The corporation counted 1,222,390 people in 291,229 households over 319.39 square kilometres. Wikipedia’s infobox gives 1,221,233, a small conflict; the census series for the wider city area gives 1,343,402, which is the figure for the whole Vasai taluka. Of the taluka’s people, 1,230,140 were urban and only 113,262 rural. Outside the corporation the census still counted fifty rural villages, almost all in the east and north-east, under the Tungar and Tansa hills, and a census town at Chandrapada with 7,750 people. [n12; n06; census2011.co.in; citypopulation.de; Wikipedia] Wikipedia calls Vasai-Virar the fifth-largest city in Maharashtra. [n12] A student project from Delft, seen in summary, put Nallasopara alone at about 564,664 people on 43 square kilometres. [n31; TU Delft repository] If that is right, the place that the Gazetteer of 1882 had called a town of 1,700 people now held more than half a million.

By religion, the taluka was 78.05 per cent Hindu, 8.45 per cent Muslim, 8.25 per cent Christian, 2.53 per cent Buddhist and 1.75 per cent Jain. [n22; n06; Wikipedia: Vasai taluka, citing Census Table C-01] The Christian share, about 110,800 people, was the highest anywhere in the Mumbai region, the legacy of the Portuguese centuries. [n34; n06] By language the city was 46.49 per cent Marathi, 26.58 per cent Hindi, 9.26 per cent Gujarati, 3.10 per cent Bhojpuri, 2.72 per cent Urdu and 1.78 per cent Marwari; the taluka as a whole was 49.97 per cent Marathi. [n22] Those figures come from Wikipedia and were not checked against the census tables themselves. No census was held in 2021, and the research found no reliable estimate of the population in 2026. The notes say that a plausible figure is well above two million, and warn against quoting any number without a source. [n06, §1 Inferences]

The second bishop and the Bible

In December 2009 the Diocese of Vasai had installed its second bishop, Felix Anthony Machado, born at Remedy in 1948, who came from Nashik after a decade in the Vatican’s office for interreligious dialogue. [n38; n26] In 2010 Fr D’Britto’s Subodh Bible appeared, the Marathi translation with introductions and about two hundred illustrations on which he had worked since about 1997; Vatican Radio called it an instant hit. [n38; Vatican Radio, 3 Aug 2010] In 2013 it won the state’s translation award, and in 2014 the Sahitya Akademi award, reported as the first Bible translation to win a national prize. In 2015 D’Britto received the Dnyanoba-Tukaram Puraskar. [n38]

A marathon

In 2011 the corporation began a road race, the Vasai-Virar Mayor’s Marathon. The first edition had about seven hundred participants. [n29; Wikipedia (mirror)] Over the next fifteen years it would draw celebrity guests such as Sachin Tendulkar and Riteish Deshmukh, and by its twelfth edition, on 8 December 2024, about fifteen thousand runners, flagged off by the wrestler Sakshi Malik and welcomed by Hitendra Thakur. [n06; n29; Lokmat Times] It became, with the December Kala-Krida Mahotsav, one of the two great civic rituals of the new city, both of them, by the account of their organisers and their critics alike, closely tied to the ruling party.

On 5 January 2012 Narayan Mankar became mayor, serving until June 2015. He was the man who had lost the Vasai assembly seat to Vivek Pandit in 2009. [n26; n29]

The line north

For a century and a half the history of this coast had run along the railway, and in these sixteen years the railway changed more than in the fifty before.

16 April 2013: Dahanu

Until 2013 the electric suburban locals ran only as far as Virar, sixty kilometres from Churchgate. North of Virar, passengers changed to MEMU trains; in March 2013 eighteen MEMU services a day ran between Virar and Dahanu. Western Railway had announced the extension of EMU services to Dahanu Road in 2002. On 16 April 2013 the Railway Minister, Pawan Kumar Bansal, flagged off the first EMU between Churchgate and Dahanu Road, and services began the next day. Railway enthusiasts’ forums give the first-day count as fifteen or twenty services. [n29; Wikipedia: Western line (search summary); railenquiry.in forum (unofficial)] For the first time Virar was not the end of the line. It had become a through station, and the commuter belt stretched beyond the Vaitarna into Palghar’s rice country.

25 December 2017: the air-conditioned local

On Christmas Day 2017 Mumbai’s first air-conditioned suburban train began running, first between Churchgate and Borivali. From 1 January 2018 it ran a regular Churchgate-Virar service, eight of its twelve daily services fast, stopping at Vasai Road and Bhayandar among others. Sources disagree on boarding rules and on whether the fare was 1.2 or 1.3 times the first-class fare. [n29; PIB, Ministry of Railways; Tribune; Scroll (all via search summary)] The research notes draw the obvious line: the Dahanu extension and the AC local both stretched Mumbai’s network beyond Vasai-Virar, so that the corporation’s area became a transit corridor rather than a terminus. [n29, §3 Inferences]

Fifteen coaches and two new lines

The great constraint was track. Locals and long-distance trains shared the lines north of Borivali, and the Virar locals, among the most crowded trains in India, could not be lengthened or multiplied without more of it. The notes did not find when the first twelve-car locals ran on the Virar route, nor the approval history of the new lines. [n29, §3 Gaps] What they found is the result.

On 19 January 2026 the fifth and sixth lines between Kandivali and Borivali opened for operations. [n06; Curly Tales, Jan 2026] North of Borivali, the 26-kilometre extension of those lines to Virar, costing 2,184 crore rupees under the third phase of the Mumbai Urban Transport Project, was about thirty per cent complete by August 2026, with completion targeted for 2029. Its purpose is to separate the suburban locals from the long-distance trains. In July 2026 the Mumbai Railway Vikas Corporation invited bids for its overhead electrification, a thirty-six-month contract. [n06; Curly Tales, Aug 2026; Rail Asia; Metro Rail News] In September 2026 Western Railway announced two new platforms at Borivali, including a 330-metre Platform 3A for Borivali-Virar locals, to add thirty to forty services towards Virar, with new platforms planned at Andheri and Virar too. [n06; FPJ, Sept 2026]

At Virar itself, the quadrupling work on the line to Dahanu widened Platforms 3A and 4A from 6.5 metres to 10.5 metres in April 2026 and extended them to take fifteen-coach trains instead of twelve. The Free Press Journal published “first photos” of fifteen-coach rakes arriving at the Virar carshed. [n29; Lokmat Times, Apr 2026; FPJ] From 1 May 2026 Western Railway ran fifteen-coach suburban services on the Virar-Dahanu Road section. [n06; n29]

Behind these numbers is a human cost that the notes could not measure. No source gave overcrowding figures or annual deaths on the tracks between Virar and Nallasopara. [n06, Gaps] A Marathi report from November 2026 counts five deaths in twenty-four hours on the Vasai-Vaitarna stretch, four in accidents and one natural. [n29; Aapla Mahanagar] And one young life shows what the train meant in the other direction. Ayush Mhatre, born on 16 July 2007 (Wikipedia says in Nallasopara; one report says Virar), travelled about forty-six kilometres each way by train from Virar to Churchgate for cricket practice. In 2026 he captained India to its sixth under-19 World Cup title. [n27; Wikipedia: Ayush Mhatre]

1 August 2014: Palghar district

Since the British carved the Konkan into collectorates, Bassein had been a taluka of Thana district. On 1 August 2014 that ended. Palghar became Maharashtra’s thirty-sixth district, separated from Thane, with eight talukas: Palghar, Vada, Vikramgad, Jawhar, Mokhada, Dahanu, Talasari and Vasai. The state cabinet had approved it on 13 June 2014. The first collector, named in July, was Bangar. [n29; Wikipedia: Palghar district, citing mid-day, 7 Aug 2014; The News Minute; Business Standard, 23 Jul 2014] (Wikipedia’s infobox gives a date of 21 August 2016, flagged “citation needed”; it is wrong.)

So Vasai, the old capital of the Portuguese Province of the North and the richest taluka of Thana, became the southernmost and by far the most populous taluka of a new district whose headquarters lay fifty kilometres north, in a district whose other talukas are largely Adivasi hill and forest country. The district’s later collectors would deal with the flood relief, the port protests and the fishing surveys that fill the rest of this chapter.

The parliamentary seat had been Palghar since 2009. Its members tell the political story in brief: Baliram Jadhav of the BVA in 2009; Chintaman Vanaga of the BJP in 2014; Rajendra Gavit of the BJP in a 2018 by-election, then of the Shiv Sena in 2019; and Hemant Savara of the BJP in 2024, with 601,244 votes, while the BVA’s Rajesh Patil came third with 254,517. [n26; Wikipedia: Palghar Lok Sabha]

The BVA at its height, 2014–2019

In the assembly elections of October 2014 the Thakurs took back everything they had lost in 2009. In Vasai, Hitendra Thakur, now standing for the BVA, beat Vivek Pandit by 31,896 votes, 97,291 to 65,395. In Nallasopara, Kshitij Thakur won 113,566 votes, 50.72 per cent, and beat Rajan Naik of the BJP by 54,499 on a 57 per cent turnout. [n29; Wikipedia: Vasai AC; Nallasopara AC] Vilas Tare held Boisar for the BVA. [n26] That year Hitendra Thakur’s election affidavit listed eight criminal cases, charges framed in seven, none ending in conviction. [n29; n26]

In the 2015 corporation election the BVA won 106 of the 115 seats. [n06; FPJ] (Wikipedia’s text says 2017; the notes prefer the newspaper’s 2015.) The Association for Democratic Reforms recorded that Hitendra Thakur declared assets of 158 crore rupees, the highest of any candidate, and his cousin Pankaj Thakur 128 crore. [n26; ADR] On 29 June 2015 Pravina Thakur, Hitendra’s wife, became mayor, serving until 28 December 2017; she is called the first woman mayor. [n26; Wikipedia: VVCMC] She was followed by Rupesh Jadhav from 29 December 2017 to 25 July 2019; by Narayan Mankar again for twenty-eight days, 26 July to 22 August 2019; and by Pravin Shetty from 23 August 2019 to 28 June 2020. [n26] The notes suggest that the brief 2019 return of Mankar may reflect a gap-filling arrangement rather than a political story. [n26, Inferences] Deputy mayors in the period include Prakash Rodrigues, the fourth to hold the post. [n26]

A state notification of 21 February 2015 sought to make the corporation the planning authority for twenty-one villages; a later joint committee of the National Green Tribunal describes it as the Special Planning Authority for the sub-region. [n29; VVCMC DCR (search summary); NGT joint committee report] Planning powers, which CIDCO had held since 1990, were passing to the city.

In October 2019 the family held its ground again. Kshitij Thakur won Nallasopara with 149,868 votes, 56.37 per cent, beating Pradeep Sharma of the Shiv Sena by 43,729. Hitendra Thakur beat Vijay Patil of the Shiv Sena in Vasai by 25,995. Rajesh Patil won Boisar for the BVA. [n29; n26] The notes call the 2014 and 2019 results the BVA “at its peak”, both seats held by margins of 25,000 to 55,000, “just before the 2024 collapse”. [n29, §3 Inferences]

After June 2020 there was no mayor. Civic elections across Maharashtra were delayed, and none was held in Vasai-Virar between 2020 and January 2026. The corporation was presumably run by an administrator in those years, though the notes did not find a source saying so. [n06; n26, Gaps]

Bridges, flyovers and corridors

The southern gate of the coast is the Vasai creek, and the creek was bridged again.

The first road bridge over the creek on the Mumbai-Ahmedabad highway had been built in 1968. As it weakened, the National Highways Authority began a second four-lane bridge alongside it in 2018, at a reported cost of more than 247 crore rupees. Its 918-metre span, a cantilever, with 1.33 kilometres of approach roads, 2.25 kilometres in all, opened on 27 March 2023. A Free Press Journal headline called it a new bridge “after a decade of misery”. [n12; Mumbai Live; Swarajya; FPJ] An infrastructure database says a 1.5-kilometre bridge opened in 2020, which conflicts; the notes advise checking before use, and the book gives the March 2023 date from the better sources. [n29, §3 Gaps; infralens] A Bhayandar-Vasai creek bridge, proposed by MMRDA as a six-lane crossing and in later reports as a double-deck bridge carrying a metro line, remained only a proposal; its estimated cost rose in the reports from 1,100 crore to 2,500 crore rupees. [n29; FPJ, 25 Feb 2020; Swarajya]

Inside the city, the Narangi (Naringi) flyover at Virar, linking Virar East and West and expected to save thirty minutes, was opened by the district’s Guardian Minister, Ganesh Naik, on a Friday reported on 8 May; the year is not in the sources, and the notes think it probably 2026 but cannot confirm it. [n29; FPJ; Mumbai Live]

Larger schemes stood on paper. The state cabinet approved a 126.3-kilometre Virar-Alibaug Multimodal Corridor on a build-operate-transfer model, its first phase running 96.41 kilometres from Navghar to Balavali near Pen; as of September 2026 the state road corporation was awaiting viability-gap funding from the Centre, with construction “likely by March 2027”. An aggregator claims preliminary works began in February 2026, which is weaker evidence. [n06; FPJ, Sept 2026; Lokmat Times; Blackridge Research] An Uttan-Virar sea link, a 24.35-kilometre sea bridge, was at the planning stage; MMRDA began acquiring about 104 hectares at Uttan in June 2026, and the link received in-principle approval in August 2026 to connect with the Delhi-Mumbai Expressway near Virar, according to an aggregator. [n06; InfraCapitalist] A local site reported in August 2026 that an offshore international airport for Palghar district was under feasibility study; the notes rate it low confidence. [n06; vasaivirar.org]

The notes offer a careful projection: together, the corridor, the sea link, the bullet train and the Vadhvan port could turn Virar from a commuter dormitory at the end of the line into a junction of freight and road corridors. It is, they add, still a projection. [n06, §2 Inferences]

Water from the Surya

For the first thirteen years of the corporation, water remained what it had been in the 1990s: scarce, and bought from tankers. Around 2012, about 1.3 million people depended on tankers and bore wells, and tankers moved about fifteen million litres a day from bore wells along the highway. [n17; Vikalp Sangam] The existing schemes, Pelhar, Usgaon, Papadkhind and the disputed Maswan figure, were listed in the 2014 environmental papers for a new project as the city’s only supply. [n29; Surya summary]

That project was the Surya Regional Bulk Water Supply Scheme, built by MMRDA to bring water from the Surya river system in the north of the district. It was designed to supply 403 million litres a day in all: 185 for Vasai-Virar and 218 for Mira-Bhayandar. Phase 1 was commissioned in June 2023, and water reached Vasai-Virar in November 2023, released on the order of Chief Minister Eknath Shinde without a formal inauguration. Prime Minister Narendra Modi dedicated the scheme on 12 January 2024. [n06; Indian Infrastructure, 23 Nov 2023 and 17 Jan 2024; L&T News; Mumbai Live]

It eased the crisis. It did not end it. A BVA legislator, in an older report, claimed the city received about 230 million litres a day against a need of about 372. [n29; ThePrint] The Kholsapada-2 dam, costing 135 crore rupees, was over eighty per cent complete in 2026, with 22 million litres a day expected, and MMRDA’s Deharji project was planned to add 190. [n29; FPJ; PropNewsTime] On 15 November 2026, three days before this book’s research closed, the Marathi daily Sakal warned that reservoir levels were falling and scarcity was feared, and that Vasai-Virar’s historic lakes were choked with garbage and water hyacinth through neglected maintenance. [n06; eSakal, 15 Nov 2026]

The old water system had its defenders. Volunteers of the Yuva Vikas Sanstha, the group that had counted the ponds in 2013, began restoring bavkhals. Where they did, the dissolved salts in nearby wells fell from 750 to 300 parts per million. [n17; Vikalp Sangam, 2019] It was a small demonstration of what the old gardeners had known: the ponds kept the salt out.

The bullet train and the port

Two national projects in the north of the new district provoked the largest protests on this coast since the green-belt rallies, and in both the protesters were the descendants of the same communities, farmers and fishers, who had built the coast.

The bullet train

When Prime Minister Modi and Japan’s Prime Minister Shinzo Abe launched the Mumbai-Ahmedabad high-speed rail project in September 2017, farmers at Boisar staged a symbolic protest. In May 2018 about a thousand tribal farmers protested at Azad Maidan in Mumbai. The project needed 353 hectares in Maharashtra, mostly in Palghar district, and the farmers argued that the consent of village councils required under the 1996 law extending self-rule to scheduled areas had not been obtained. The Uddhav Thackeray government later paused acquisition pending a petition from Gujarat. [n29; DT Next; Tribune; Mongabay India, Oct 2019] By March 2024 land acquisition for the corridor was complete, and a Virar high-speed station was under construction. [n06; Wikipedia: Virar high-speed railway station] The notes found no detail on protests or the Virar station after 2020. [n29, Gaps]

Vadhvan

The larger fight was over the sea.

On 30 August 2024 Prime Minister Modi laid the foundation stone of the Vadhvan (Vadhavan) port in Dahanu taluka, north of Virar in the same district, a deep-water container port costing about 76,000 crore rupees, mostly through public-private partnership. [n29; n06; All India Radio; India Sea Trade News] Fishers protested at the groundbreaking itself. A fishing leader said the port would reclaim about 5,000 acres of sea and add a restricted zone of about 30,000 acres; research cited by the protesters said it would directly hit sixteen fishing villages in Palghar. [n17; FPJ] One gathering drew more than eight thousand villagers; a Free Press Journal headline spoke of “25,000 fishermen” protesting. The Dahanu Taluka Environment Protection Authority was reported to have rejected the proposal as “wholly impermissible” and “illegal”; the source gives no date for that decision. Activists said no legal procedure had been followed. [n29; FPJ; Behanbox] The Koliwadas of the whole coast, from Cuffe Parade in Mumbai to Zai in the north, Pachubandar among them, shut down in protest; their leaders said nine to ten thousand boat owners in Mumbai, Thane and Palghar would be affected. [n20; Lokmat (snippet)]

The state pressed on. In December 2025 the Palghar collector, Dr Indu Rani Jakhar, called for a full social and livelihood survey of the fishers affected. [n17; FPJ] The port’s chairman reviewed land acquisition, road links and fishermen’s concerns. [n06; FPJ] In 2026 the Bombay High Court cleared the removal of mangroves for the project. [n06; FPJ] In May 2026 the cost was given as 76,220 crore rupees, and in November 2026 the port signed agreements worth 5,700 crore, with its first phase targeted for 2029. [n06; India Sea Trade News, May and Nov 2026]

The fishers’ sea

Vadhvan arrived on top of a fishery already in trouble. Vasai’s fishers had moved from many species caught in traditional bag nets to dependence on pomfret, and even pomfret was falling. [n17] A 2017 state resolution banned LED lights, purse seining and gill netting by mechanised boats in certain waters; traditional fishers demanded a special investigation into illegal purse-seine and LED fishing. [n08; FPJ] An ONGC seismic survey closed Vasai’s waters to fishing for fifty-six days. [n20; Lokmat] In January 2023 Sakal reported that cold weather and climate change had cut fish landings at the district’s harbours, Arnala, Pachubandar and Naigaon among them. [n20; eSakal, 19 Jan 2023]

The sea was dangerous as well as poorer. When Cyclone Tauktae approached in May 2021, “thousands of boats” gathered on Vasai’s beaches, and some sheltered on the Gujarat and Diu coasts. [n20; Lokmat] A Pachubandar boat had hit rocks off the harbour, around 2014, with losses of 25 to 30 lakh rupees, its crew safe. [n20; Lokmat] Early in 2026 a Pachubandar boat coming in saw a mysterious rotating “water ring” at sea and turned away; the Fisheries Department alerted the Coast Guard and the Navy. [n20; Pudhari] And one story from the Marathi press, undated, shows the fishers’ own code: a Vasai boat owner who found a whale, a devmasa, the “god-fish” that the Gazetteer of 1882 recorded the Son Kolis would not eat, caught in his net, cut the net and let it go, accepting the loss. [n20; Lokmat; n08; Gazetteer 1882, p. 148]

The green belt, contested again

The plan fight of the 1990s had never quite ended. In the late 2010s it came back, and this time the land in question was the salt pans.

MMRDA’s draft Regional Plan for 2016–2036 proposed to reclassify 1,560 acres of former salt pans in Vasai, previously a no-development zone, as a “growth centre” for residential, sports, educational and commercial use. It would also allow fifteen-metre buildings in most green zones and twenty-four-metre buildings in the old village cores, the gaothans. Residents pointed out that the salt-pan land floods every monsoon. The activist Samir Vartak said about 63,000 local people objected; The Quint reported at least 40,000 objections from Vasai-Virar and more than 62,000 from across the region. Residents wrote to Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis, around 2018. They credited the green zone with keeping Vasai less polluted than Mumbai. [n17; n06; ICSF/Quint; CAT] The notes call the 1,560-acre block the single biggest land-use fight on record here, and could not confirm its fate in the sanctioned plan. [n17, Inferences and Gaps] Residents also opposed a proposed dumping ground “in our midst”. [n06; HT via Magzter]

They had reason to fear the water. Around 2018 residents said mangroves had been cleared from more than a hundred acres for buildings, and that the sea was moving further onto the land every day. [n17; ICSF/Quint] During one monsoon around 2017, a village was under water for five or six days and at least six people drowned. [n17; ICSF/Quint]

July 2018

In July 2018 the rain came hard, and the low eastern ground went under. In Nallasopara, residents of Achole Road and Chheda Nagar were stranded for five days. Vishal Nagar, H and C Colony, Vidyamandir Road, Tulinj Road and Achole were submerged. [n31; NewsBytes; Lokmat Times] In Vasai tehsil about four hundred people were rescued from the salt pans. [n13; FPJ via PressReader, 10 Jul 2018] The environmentalist Debi Goenka of the Conservation Action Trust said wetland reclamation in Thane and Vasai had “shrunk the natural drainage”; Stalin Dayanand of Vanashakti called Vasai-Virar natural floodplains with widespread wetlands and salt pans, unsuited to townships built on raised fill; more than sixty per cent of Thane’s wetlands were gone. [n17; Asian Age via CAT, 13 Jul 2018] A report by NEERI and IIT Bombay followed; its recommendations, according to later reporting, were never carried out. [n17; n06; Deccan Herald, 2025]

The research notes put the connection at its plainest: the flood story is the land story in reverse. The same low ground that was paddy, salt pan and bavkhal now holds buildings, and the water that drained into the ponds now stands on roads and tracks. They add, honestly, that the causal link is argued by activists and the railway, not by a published study the research found. [n17, Inferences]

The argument went to the courts. A climate activist, Charan Bhatt, petitioned the National Green Tribunal against debris dumping in Vasai-Virar’s wetlands, citing an ISRO remote-sensing study of wetland and mangrove decline, around 2024. [n06; n26; FPJ] A joint committee reported to the Tribunal in a Vasai case, OA 201/2023. [n06; NGT PDF] The water-policy network SANDRP recorded assaults on mangroves and wetlands at the Vasai creek in 2025. [n17; SANDRP, 8 Feb 2026] Permission was given to fell 209 mangroves near the Vasai creek for a power line, against an initial estimate of 79, according to an opinion piece. [n17; FPJ] In November 2025 Mongabay published “Uncertainty around future of Mumbai’s last green lung”, which the notes judge likely to concern the Vasai green belt but did not fetch. [n06]

Tungareshwar

On the eastern side, the sanctuary was squeezed. The first scientific study of its leopards, from February to July 2016 by Nikit Surve of the Wildlife Conservation Society, confirmed five leopards, against an estimated thirty-five in Sanjay Gandhi National Park. [n17; DNA; CAT] Activists estimated at least twenty thousand illegal constructions between Kaman and Virar East along the sanctuary’s edge. [n08; HT via PressReader, 28 Apr 2024] In a Tribunal case, the forest department said the Balayogi Sadanandbaba ashram had encroached on sanctuary land; in April 2023 Chief Minister Shinde promised to resolve the ashram’s problems. [n08; n10; NGT affidavit; Lokmat Times] At Chinchoti, in the Tungareshwar foothills, a monsoon waterfall drew crowds and took lives: three deaths in twenty-four hours in July 2023, two Goregaon college students drowned in July 2025 despite restrictions. [n12; Mid-day; Lokmat Times]

Nallasopara in the headlines, August 2018

In August 2018 the name Nallasopara reached the national press for a reason that had nothing to do with floods. What follows is a summary of a prosecution that had not ended when this book was written.

The Maharashtra Anti-Terrorism Squad raided the home and shop of a man named Vaibhav Raut in Nallasopara, and said it had seized twenty crude bombs, two gelatin sticks, four electronic and twenty-two non-electronic detonators, and 150 grams of explosive powder. Raut, about forty, was described as a coordinator of the Hindu Govansh Raksha Samiti. Sharad Kalaskar was arrested with him; Sudhanva Gondhalekar of Pune and Shrikant Pangarkar were also held; later about ten or eleven pistols were reported recovered. The ATS alleged a plot against the Sunburn music festival in Pune. Charges were brought under the anti-terror law and the Arms and Explosives Acts; a chargesheet in December 2018 named twelve accused, and the National Investigation Agency later took over. Raut’s associates said he had been framed, and the organisations Hindu Janajagruti Samiti and Sanatan Sanstha alleged a frame-up; those are contested claims. Totals of what was seized conflict across reports. [n06; n31; Business Standard/PTI; The Wire; Tribune; DNA; Madhyamam] The Bombay High Court later granted Raut bail after more than five years in jail, noting that the trial was unlikely to end soon. [n06; FPJ] No verdict is recorded in the sources. He is an accused, not a convicted man.

The notes observe that the national press tied the town’s name to the case, but that the sources describe suspects arrested there, not a district crime record. [n31, Inferences]

January 2020: a priest at the head of Marathi letters

In January 2020, just before the pandemic, Fr Francis D’Britto presided over the 93rd Akhil Bharatiya Marathi Sahitya Sammelan, the annual all-India gathering of Marathi literature, held at Osmanabad; he had been elected its president on 10 January. He was described as the first Christian elected to that office. [n38; n29; FPJ; GKToday] For a coast whose Christians had been called “Portuguese” by their neighbours for two centuries, and whose own dialects the Bombay Gazetteer had dismissed as “incorrect Marathi”, it was a moment of recognition. The man who had translated the Bible into plain Marathi stood at the head of the language’s literary world.

2020: the pandemic

Then COVID-19 arrived, and it found in the railway towns exactly the conditions in which it spread fastest.

Nallasopara was called the worst-affected town in the belt, with shared toilets, the legacy of the chawls, worsening the spread. [n29; Deccan Herald] A report found a six-fold rise in cases in the corporation over forty days, linked to commuters. The corporation reported a 55 per cent positivity rate in April 2020. As of 6 May the city’s hospitals had seven ventilators and two ICU beds. In June, eighteen areas went under a fourteen-day lockdown. [n29; Deccan Herald; FPJ; Mumbai Live] On 20 July 2020 the corporation passed ten thousand cases, 10,017 with 192 deaths, the fifth civic body in the region to do so; by late August there were 16,234 cases and 345 deaths. [n29; Mumbai Live; FPJ] The first suspected COVID death within the city’s limits was a forty-two-year-old resident of Naigaon; the date is not given. [n29; newsband.in]

The institutions of the old coast took part. St Gonsalo Garcia College in Vasai, founded by the Church in 1984, became a 750-bed COVID care centre. [n38; Wikipedia] The Kala-Krida Mahotsav was cancelled for 2020, the only break in its run, and held again from 26 to 31 December 2021 under COVID rules. [n20; eSakal] At the bottom of the economy, an eighteen-person group of Katkari Adivasis who had migrated to Bhatane village in Vasai block to work the brick kilns was reported on during the lockdown by the People’s Archive of Rural India, under the title “And miles to go before they sleep, or eat”. [n17; PARI] In April 2020 the Palghar district saw a mob lynching at Gadchinchle, outside Vasai taluka; the notes gathered no detail. [n29]

One Vasai life gives the pandemic a different ending. Harshvardhan Joshi, born in Vasai in 1995, a mountaineer and triathlete who had climbed Stok Kangri solo and unsupported at twenty, summited Everest on 23 May 2021, on his first attempt, in a carbon-neutral expedition, days after recovering from COVID-19. [n27; Wikipedia; FPJ; Yahoo News]

23 April 2021: the hospital fire

In the second wave of the pandemic, at the Vijay Vallabh Hospital in Virar, a fire broke out in the intensive-care unit early on 23 April 2021. It killed thirteen COVID patients according to Wikipedia and most wire reports; other reports said fourteen or fifteen, and The Hindu’s headline gave fifteen. The fire brigade suspected a short circuit in an air-conditioning unit. The hospital’s fire safety certificate had expired in March. Its chief executive and chief administrative officer, Dr Dilip Shah and Dr Shailesh Pathak, were arrested, and the directors were booked for culpable homicide not amounting to murder. A Vasai sessions court rejected their bail; the date was not captured. [n29; Wikipedia: Virar hospital fire; FPJ; Udayavani; Legiteye] The notes found no record of the trial’s outcome or of compensation. [n29, Gaps] As matters stand in the sources, the accused face charges that have not been decided.

Flowers in a hotter climate

In the gardens, the 2020s brought a new enemy: the weather itself.

The sonchafa growers of Arnala, who had prospered since the late 1980s, saw their yields roughly halve after unseasonal rain, the humidity and fungus that followed Cyclone Mandous in December 2022, and fewer hours of light. Subhash Bhatte of Satpale, who had produced about 11,000 flowers a day from one acre and earned about 75,000 rupees a month, found his output falling to a few thousand a day. Flower growers have no crop insurance and no access to Kisan Credit Card loans. Waterlogging, the reporter noted, had worsened as open fields vanished. [n17; 101Reporters, 7 Jan 2023] The same report records the corporation’s formation in 2009 as the moment the farmland began giving way to housing, offices and malls. [n17]

There were new crops too. The Marathi press reported avocado farming as a success in Vasai, and Vasai’s white onion selling in Mumbai at 140 to 170 rupees a bunch. [n20; Pudhari; Lokmat] Vasai’s bananas, a Saamana headline said, would go overseas, with help for growers from Jain Irrigation. [n20; Saamana]

And the dried banana had found its champion. Pius Alex Rodrigues of Nandakhal-Parsav, Sakal reported in June 2026, “spread the name of sukeli, Vasai’s famous dry banana, across India and abroad”. He died on 19 June 2026, and his family donated his eyes and skin. [n20; eSakal, Jun 2026] The notes see in him a fitting figure: a Christian farmer-entrepreneur from Nandakhal who carried to the world the snack that pilgrims buy at the Hindu fair of Nirmal. [n20, Inferences]

2023: an acquittal, a desecration, a bridge and water

The year 2023 closed one long story and opened others.

On 24 May 2023 a special TADA court in Pune, Judge S. R. Navander presiding, acquitted Jayendra “Bhai” Thakur and others of the murder of Suresh Dube, who had been shot on the Nallasopara station platform on 9 October 1989. The court found the evidence insufficient. The Free Press Journal called it the last TADA case in India; Lokmat’s headline said Bhai Thakur was acquitted “after 34 years”. Reports differ on whether two or three co-accused were acquitted with him; one search summary names Deepak Thakur and Gajanan Patil. Dube’s daughter Neha said the family would appeal to the Supreme Court. [n29; n26; FPJ; Lokmat Times; ThePrint] No outcome of an appeal is recorded in the notes. In law, as this book goes to press, the man accused for thirty-four years of ordering the killing that opened the Thakur era stands acquitted.

On 11 August 2023 the church of St Gonsalo Garcia at Gass, the diocese’s major shrine, was desecrated. Vatican News reported it, with the line that Garcia is “India’s first saint”. [n38; Vatican News, Aug 2023] The notes give no detail of what was done or by whom, and record it as the most recent attack on a church that they found. [n38]

On 27 March 2023 the new Vasai creek bridge opened. In June 2023 the first phase of the Surya water scheme was commissioned, and in November its water reached Vasai-Virar. [n12; n06]

In February 2023, the clergy of the diocese told Mid-day that more people should know about “India’s first saint”. [n09; Mid-day, 12 Feb 2023]

2024: the death of a priest, the change of a bishop

The year 2024 was the year the period’s two long stories, the Church’s and the Thakurs’, both turned.

On 6 June 2024 Bishop Felix Machado’s tenure ended. [n26; n38; GCatholic] On 15 June the Mobai Gaothan Panchayat, an East Indian body, asked Cardinal Oswald Gracias of Bombay to use the East Indian dialect in Mass and hymns, to promote devotion to St Gonsalo Garcia, to create an East Indian Bhavan at the Goregaon seminary, to simplify certification as Other Backward Classes, to put the community’s name on baptism certificates, and to preserve its wayside crucifixes and village cores. [n34; FPJ, 16 Jun 2024] The demands came from the Bombay side of the community and do not mention Kupari or Kadodi, but they describe the same anxieties: a dialect slipping away, villages swallowed, an identity that needed paper to survive.

On 25 July 2024 Fr Francis D’Britto died at Nandakhal. [n29; n26] His life had spanned the whole arc of these last two chapters. Born in 1943, by the more common date, he had edited the Marathi monthly Suvarta from 1983 to 2007; convened the Harit Vasai Saurakshan Samiti from 1989; led the tanker fight and the rally of 26 January 1992; spent about thirteen years translating the Bible into Marathi; won the state’s translation award and the Sahitya Akademi award; and presided over the Marathi Sahitya Sammelan in 2020. He wrote more than fifty books. [n38; n27; n13; Wikipedia; ThePrint; SabrangIndia; News On Air] About three thousand people came to his funeral. The Free Press Journal’s obituary said that the state had limited development to 1.5 kilometres on either side of the railway line, and quoted Noel Dabre: “The greenery that you see now is because of the movement.” [n29; FPJ, 25 Jul 2024] His birth year and his age at death are given differently across the sources, 1942 or 1943, eighty to eighty-two. [n13]

On 9 November 2024 a new bishop was named, Thomas Francis D’Souza, born at Chulne, who had been rector of the Shrine of Our Lady of Remedies at Remedy from 2013 to 2019. He was consecrated on 15 December 2024. [n38; n27; Fides, 9 Nov 2024; GCatholic] On 29 December 2024 he opened the Jubilee Year 2025 at the cathedral of Our Lady of Grace, Papdi. [n38; Catholic Connect] The coast now had a third bishop born on the island, after Dabre of Bhuigaon and Machado of Remedy.

November 2024: the shift

On 23 November 2024 the results of the Maharashtra assembly election were declared, and the Thakur era in the legislature ended.

In Vasai, Sneha Dube Pandit of the BJP beat Hitendra Thakur by 3,153 votes, polling 77,553, according to the Election Commission’s round-wise figures; other outlets gave margins of about 3,275 or “over 3,700”, and the notes prefer the Commission’s figure. [n06; n26; ECI; Lokmat Times; FPJ] In Nallasopara, Rajan Naik of the BJP, whom Kshitij Thakur had beaten by 54,499 votes ten years earlier, won with 165,113 votes, 47.58 per cent, beating Kshitij Thakur’s 128,238 by 36,875. [n06; n26; Wikipedia: Nallasopara AC; ECI] In Boisar, Vilas Tare, who had won it for the BVA in 2009 and 2014, won it again, this time for the Shiv Sena. [n26] The BVA had lost every assembly seat on the coast.

The Deccan Herald headline read: “saffron wave hits bastion of Thakurs”. It quoted a local social worker who called the result a big jolt to a family that had controlled the Vasai-Virar belt for three decades. [n06; Deccan Herald] On counting day the Free Press Journal reported tension between BJP and BVA workers in Nallasopara, and cash-for-votes allegations made by the BVA against the BJP leader Vinod Tawde; those were allegations, not findings. [n26; FPJ]

Why did the bastion fall? The sources do not say, and the notes are careful. They list what lined up with the BJP’s gains: a statewide wave for the ruling alliance; the votes of north-Indian migrants, visible in the candidates’ names in 2024 and 2026; and, later, the demolitions and the federal investigation described below. But they add that no source links any of these causally to the result. [n06, §3 Inferences; n29, §4 Inferences] The new member for Vasai’s surname suggests a north-Indian origin; that, too, is an inference, and her relationship, if any, to Vivek Pandit is not established in the sources. [n26]

Hitendra Thakur, now a former legislator, welcomed fifteen thousand runners to the twelfth Mayor’s Marathon two weeks later. [n29; Lokmat Times]

Agarwal Nagari: forty-one buildings

Five days after the election, the bulldozers came to Nallasopara East.

The buildings at Agarwal Nagari, also called Lakshmi Nagar, at Achole, stood on about thirty acres of survey numbers 22 to 30, land reserved in the plan for a sewage treatment plant and a dumping ground. They had been going up since 2006. There were forty-one of them. The Bombay High Court declared them “wholly illegal and unauthorised”. [n06; n31; Land Conflict Watch; Lokmat Times] In September 2022 the Achole police had arrested a former corporator, Sitaram Gupta, for land-grabbing and for building them. [n06; n26; Land Conflict Watch] In October 2024 residents received eviction notices; the civic body said private builders had put the buildings up on reserved land, and the High Court refused relief. [n12; n31]

On 28 November 2024 the first seven buildings were demolished. The residents appealed to the Supreme Court, which rejected their plea. [n06; PropNewsTime] From 23 January 2025 the remaining thirty-four came down; one report spoke of thirty-four in a single day. A report in February 2025 said all forty-one had been razed in a twenty-two-day operation. [n06; n12; Lokmat Times; LatestLY/ANI; PropNewsTime]

How many people lost their homes is itself disputed: about two thousand residents, in one estimate; “over 3,000 families”, in another; about eight thousand residents, according to Lokmat. [n06; Land Conflict Watch; Ghar.tv; CSR Journal] A Hindi report says the buildings were demolished without rehabilitation. [n06; CSR Journal] The families had bought flats, in many cases in good faith, in buildings that had stood for up to eighteen years, and that the courts had now found should never have existed. The notes put the case at its sharpest: the buildings were on reserved land, families bought in good faith, the demolitions were court-ordered, and the federal agency would soon allege that the civic chief had led the racket, before a court freed him. [n06, §4 Inferences]

Agarwal Nagari was not unique. A 2025-era report describes Rahmat Nagar in Nallasopara as dotted with substandard illegal buildings, and a tilted building in Nallasopara forced the evacuation of three structures, leaving hundreds homeless. [n31; FPJ]

The ED case

On 13 August 2025 the Enforcement Directorate, the federal agency that investigates money laundering, arrested four men in connection with the forty-one buildings: Anil Pawar, who had been the corporation’s commissioner from January 2022 to July 2025; Y. S. Reddy, a suspended deputy director of town planning; the former corporator Sitaram Gupta; and Arun Gupta. [n06; n26; FPJ; News On AIR, 14 Aug 2025] Sources differ on the date of Pawar’s arrest, July or 13 August, and one gives a figure of 169 crore rupees. [n29]

What follows are the ED’s allegations, not facts found by any court. The agency alleged that dummy builders had been used to put up the buildings; that approval documents were fabricated; that commissions were collected through a coded system in which “C” stood for the Commissioner; and its chargesheet called Pawar the “chief controller” of illegal construction. [n06; n26; Deccan Herald; Tribune]

Then the courts spoke. In October 2025, according to several reports, a bench of the Bombay High Court led by the Chief Justice, with Justice Gautam Ankhad, held Pawar’s arrest by the ED illegal and ordered his immediate release. It called the ED’s stand “speculative and based on hazy facts” and rejected the agency’s request to stay its order. [n29; n06; UNI India; FPJ; Deccan Chronicle; National Herald; Tribune (PTI)] The month comes from a search tool’s statement and was not verified on the pages themselves. What happened next, whether the ED appealed, what became of the chargesheet, was not found. [n06, Gaps] The book states the position as the sources leave it: the High Court found the former commissioner’s arrest illegal; the allegations against him and the others have not been proved in any court.

27 August 2025: Ramabai Apartment

Two weeks after the arrests, in Virar East, a building fell down.

At about five minutes past midnight on 27 August 2025, the rear part of Ramabai Apartment, a building described in the reports as unauthorised, collapsed. It had been built in 2011–12 and had fifty flats, twelve of them in the wing that fell. A family was celebrating a one-year-old girl’s first birthday that night. The girl died, and so did her parents. Seventeen people were killed in all. [n06; n26; The Week/PTI, 28 Aug 2025; Tribune; FPJ] The builder, Nittal Sane, was arrested for culpable homicide not amounting to murder. The state gave five lakh rupees to each bereaved family. [n06; The Week/PTI] The notes record the arrest and the charge; they record no trial.

The building had stood for thirteen years. It was built in the corporation’s second year. The notes link the collapse to the demolitions not by cause but by sequence: seven months after the forty-one buildings came down because they were illegal, an illegal building came down by itself, and seventeen people died. [n06, §4 Inferences]

The monsoons of 2025

That same August the rain returned. On 19 and 20 August 2025 a red alert was declared for Mumbai, Thane and Palghar. Waterlogging between Nallasopara and Vasai stations and signal-point failures at Vasai junction disrupted the Western Railway, and 497 people were temporarily moved to safety within the corporation. A Deccan Herald headline called the city “Venice”. [n29; Deccan Herald, 20 Aug 2025] Western Railway blamed the flooding on its Vasai-Virar tracks on unplanned construction over salt pans, mangroves and waterholes in Nallasopara and Virar, on choked culverts, and on overflow from the Pelhar dam, and noted that the recommendations of the 2018 NEERI-IIT Bombay report had not been carried out. [n06; n17; PropNewsTime] In late September 2025 another red alert brought heavy waterlogging again. [n29; Deccan Herald]

On 30 November 2025 Mukesh Save, former mayor of Virar, the “architect” of the Kala-Krida Mahotsav and its main pillar for thirty-seven years, died in Dadar, aged seventy-three. [n20; eSakal]

January 2026: the whistle and the lotus

Then, after a gap of almost six years without an elected corporation, the city voted.

Polling in the Vasai-Virar corporation election took place on 15 January 2026, and the votes were counted the next day. Turnout was 57.12 per cent, one of the highest in the state. The BVA won 71 of the 115 seats; the BJP, which had held about one seat before, won 43; the Shinde faction of the Shiv Sena won one. The Uddhav Thackeray faction of the Sena, the Congress, both factions of the NCP, the Vanchit Bahujan Aghadi, AIMIM, the Aam Aadmi Party and the BSP won none. [n06; FPJ, 16 Jan 2026; India TV] Wikipedia lists the ruling side as BVA 70 plus one Congress member; an early Lokmat count had shown the BVA leading in eighty-seven seats. [n06]

The Deccan Herald headline read: “whistle blows lotus off Vasai fort”. The whistle is the BVA’s symbol; the lotus is the BJP’s. [n06; Deccan Herald] The BVA had held its city. But it had held it with seventy-one seats instead of 106, and many of its best-known figures had lost. Pankaj Thakur, Hitendra’s cousin, lost to Mehul Shah of the BJP. The former mayor Rupesh Jadhav lost Ward 21 to Vishal Jadhav of the BJP. Kiran Thakur lost to Ravi Purohit. Hardik Raut lost to Gaurav Raut. Dhananjay Gawade lost to Jayprakash Singh. [n06; FPJ] The names of several of the winners, Shah, Purohit, Singh, point to the Gujarati, Rajasthani and north-Indian communities that the train had brought; the notes say that the political weight of north-Indian voters is visible in these results, while warning that this is an inference, not a migration statistic. [n06, §1 Gaps]

The research notes read the arc as a whole: electoral defeat in November 2024, demolitions from November 2024 to February 2025, the federal case of August to October 2025, a fatal collapse in August 2025, and partial civic survival in January 2026. They add, again, that no source establishes a causal link between these events and the BJP’s rise. [n29, §4 Inferences]

On 3 February 2026 the new corporation elected its mayor and deputy mayor. The mayor was Ajiv Patil of the BVA, who had held his own seat in January. [n06; n26] Wikipedia’s mayors’ table lists him separately from Rajeev Patil, the first mayor of 2010; whether the two are related could not be confirmed. [n26, Gaps] The deputy mayor, the fifth to hold the post, was Marshal Dominic Lopes, also of the BVA. [n06; n26] The notes see in his election a sign of the Catholic community’s continuing civic presence. [n06, §6] The commissioner is named in one place on Wikipedia as Manoj Suryavanshi and in another as D. Gangadhar. [n06]

Faith in the Jubilee years

The Catholic year

Five days before the election, on Sunday 11 January 2026, the Catholics of the coast had gathered as they did every January in the ruined Jesuit church of the Holy Name inside Vasai Fort for the feast of St Gonsalo Garcia, the Killa cha Sann. Fr Peter Fernandez presided over the feast Mass, attended by hundreds. Families came the evening before and cooked East Indian dishes in enclosures among the ruins; once, the report said, they had come by bullock cart or by boat. [n38; n09; FPJ, 11 Jan 2026] The Archaeological Survey maintains the fort, and locals may hold a service there every Sunday. [n09] The feast falls on the first Sunday nearest the neap tide after Christmas, a calendar set by the sea. [n38]

The diocese had kept the Jubilee of 2025 in its own way. On 4 March 2025 Bishop Thomas D’Souza blessed Mini Jubilee Crosses for each parish, as a substitute for distant pilgrimages. [n38; Catholic Connect] In 2025 the parish community of the cathedral at Papdi received a UNESCO Asia-Pacific Award of Merit for its restoration of the sixteenth-century church. [n11; Outlook Traveller; FPJ] In July 2025 the bishop ordained the diocese’s first deacon in five years, a sign of the scarcity of vocations in a diocese that had once supplied priests to the whole north Konkan. [n38; Catholic Connect] On 1 November 2025 Simon Almeida was named Bishop of Poona; one local source gives his birthplace as Gass, which the notes could not confirm. [n38; Fides] Across the creek, Archbishop John Rodrigues had taken over Bombay on 25 January 2025 after Pope Francis accepted Cardinal Oswald Gracias’s resignation. [n38; Catholic Connect]

Pope Leo XIV declared a Year of St Francis, from 10 January 2026 to 10 January 2027, for the eight hundredth anniversary of the saint’s death. On 7 June 2026 the Diocese of Vasai designated St Joseph’s Church at Umralla, one of the Kupari villages of the old Sopara agar, a Pilgrim Centre for that year, with a plenary indulgence. [n38; Catholic Connect] It is a fitting choice. The Franciscans were the first order to build churches on this coast in 1534, Gonsalo Garcia died a Franciscan, and the priest who saved the green belt was a Franciscan. The Year of St Francis brought pilgrims to a village of the garden belt that the movement had kept green.

The diocese’s numbers, as of 31 December 2022 in the Catholic directory updated on 15 November 2026, were 117,026 Catholics, 2.6 per cent of a population of 4,439,027 across a territory of 7,596 square kilometres; 49 parishes and 4 missions; 185 priests, 102 diocesan and 83 religious; 458 religious, 103 brothers and 355 sisters; and only 4 seminarians. Other counts give 135,677 (2010) or 136,800, with different boundaries and methods, and the notes warn that the apparent decline more likely reflects different sources than a real fall. [n38; n34; GCatholic; Wikipedia; Catholics India] The notes’ summary of the diocese’s weight stands: small in share but dense in clergy, religious and schools, with the oldest Latin church community on this coast, the only Indian-born canonised saint, a bishop who served in the Vatican’s office for interreligious dialogue, and a priest who became the leading figure of Marathi letters. [n38, §6 Inferences]

One legal question remained open. In October 2026 the Free Press Journal reported that a proposal to add thirty-three Maharashtra groups, “including East Indian Catholics”, to the central list of Other Backward Classes was awaiting final approval from the Union Cabinet, according to Vicky Misquitta of the Mobai Gaothan Panchayat. The state’s list had included East Indians since 2006; the central list still did not. Neither source names Kupari or Samvedi Christians. [n34; n41; FPJ, updated 4 Oct 2026]

The Hindu year

The Hindu calendar ran alongside, and the Marathi press recorded it. On 19 November 2025 the agricultural paper Agrowon reported that Konkan root vegetables, purple yam, kanak and sweet potato, were in high demand at the Nirmal yatra “amid the rising tide of junk food”. [n20; Agrowon/eSakal] In September 2026, 1,709 Gauri images were installed across the city for the Gauri festival that follows Ganesh Chaturthi. [n20; eSakal, 17 Sep 2026] At Navratri 2026 the Shri Jarimari Mata temple at Umelman held its festival. [n20; Pudhari] The Jivdani hill drew its Navratri and Sunday crowds by its 1,375 steps and its funicular. [n20] A Vasai Ganesh mandal, “Vasai Cha Maharaja”, won a Lokmanya Mahotsav award. [n13; Yahoo News]

The Kuparis in 2026

The Kuparis entered 2026 as they had entered the century: uncounted by any census, estimated by themselves at tens of thousands, living in the garden villages of the north and in Bombay and abroad. [n34] The research notes found no Kupari-specific organisation with a verifiable name and founding date, and no reliable list of notable Kuparis. [n34, Gaps] What they found were signs of an identity kept by memory. A June 2026 article on the community’s red cap and red lugda found them “largely absent from daily life but remain part of community identity”. [n34; All About Vasai Virar, 29 Jun 2026] A Loksatta column, “Vasaitil Khristayan”, by Disha Khatu, reportedly described the traditional dress of the Samvedi (Kupari), Somvanshi (Vadval), East Indian and Koli Christians, though only its teaser could be read. [n34] A 2019 polemical article by a local Hindu writer argued that the coast’s villages remained “highly segregated socially and communally”, with each Catholic caste group keeping its own organisations and rarely intermarrying, while urban areas were not; the same writer said he knew of no communal incident in his lifetime. [n07; n41; Swarajya, 5 Mar 2019] An East Indian blog said there was “hardly any stratification according to caste”; the two views are both recorded. [n07]

The great Kupari villages, Bhuigaon, Wagholi, Gass, Umrale, Bolinj, Nandakhal, Mardes, Rajodi, were by 2026 inside the corporation, and so absent from the census village tables. [n11] Their churches, their bavkhal, and their surviving orchards were what the green belt had kept.

The Vadvals in 2026

The Vadvals, too, could not be counted. The last caste census was in 1931; Enthoven had put the Vadval division at about 8,000 around 1920; a missionary database’s figures of 47,000 Vadvals and 528,000 Somvanshis are of unknown method and unreliable. [n41, §13] Wikipedia relays a Maharashtra Times claim that the Vadvals are the most numerous community in Palghar district, unverified. [n41] Their identity was split between Hindu Vadvals, the Somvanshi Kshatriya Pathare, landed and Palghar-based, and Christian Vadvals within the East Indian community. [n41] The Somvanshi Kshatriya Samaj Sangh held its annual sports festival, its forty-second edition, with branches at Bordi-Gholvad, Dehari and Jhai-Borigaon; the Vadval Samaj website offered directories, matrimony and blood-donor services. [n41; vadvalsamaj.com] The notes see in these sites and festivals a revival of identity alongside the loss of the spoken dialect. [n41, Inferences] The Vadval dialect, Phudagi, was listed by Glottolog in its 2026 edition as “threatened”. [n41; Glottolog] The People’s Linguistic Survey of India had recorded its sound shifts in 2017, sakhar to hakar for sugar, shimpi to himpi for tailor, and reported that the community was recording and printing its folk songs for fear the language would vanish. [n41; indiandistricts.in, relaying PLSI 2017]

July 2026: the flood

In the first week of July 2026 the worst flood in the sources fell on the coast.

From 4 to 8 July heavy rain flooded the district. On 7 July the Palghar collector, Dr Indu Rani Jakhar, reported an average of 203 millimetres of rain in twenty-four hours. Ten people had died in rain-related incidents in Palghar district since 1 July, and one was missing in the Surya river near Dahanu. Three hundred and eighty-nine families, 1,261 people, were moved to relief centres in Palghar, Vasai and Wada talukas. Teams of the National and State Disaster Response Forces were deployed in Vasai and Virar. [n29; FPJ, 7 Jul 2026] By 13 July nine rain-related deaths had been reported in Vasai-Virar alone; the article’s own count is internally inconsistent, six plus four more bodies. A man’s body was found in the Waluv pond in Vasai East. [n29; The Quint, 13 Jul 2026] On one day, services between Vasai Road and Virar were suspended when the tracks flooded near Nallasopara, fast trains ran forty to fifty minutes late, and angry passengers surrounded a locomotive pilot at Vasai station. [n29; FPJ] In Nallasopara the flood went where it always went: Tulinj, Vijay Nagar and Giriz under knee-deep water; Tulinj police station under about two feet; the station tracks submerged; an Achole Gaon resident describing water in his hall, kitchen and bedroom. [n31; FPJ; Lokmat Times; DNA]

The rainfall figures conflict, because they measure different things over different periods. One report recorded 690 millimetres across the city’s divisions in forty-eight hours; a Lokmat Times piece is said to report more than a thousand millimetres in the first week of July; the collector’s figure is 203 millimetres in twenty-four hours, averaged over the district. The notes warn against combining them. [n29; n31; FPJ; Lokmat Times; Bombay Samachar] Developers, Lokmat reported, began promoting “waterlogging-free” housing projects. [n29; Lokmat Times]

The July 2026 flood killed more people than any earlier Vasai flood for which the research found a death toll. But it found no tolls at all for 2005, 2018, 2019, 2021 or 2024, so the comparison cannot honestly be made. [n29, §4 Inferences]

On 13 September 2026, on the eve of Ganpati, overnight rain flooded Nallasopara East’s flower market and main market near the station, under an orange alert. [n29; FPJ, 13 Sep 2026] The flowers of Arnala, brought in for the festival, stood in water in the market built on the old salt waste.

Sport, dance and the screen

If the politics and the floods were the city’s burden, its young people were its pride, and the sources about them are among the happiest in this book.

Cricketers of the Virar local

Two boys from the railway towns captained India to under-19 World Cups, eight years apart. Prithvi Shaw, born in Virar in 1999 to migrant parents from Gaya, scored 546 in a Harris Shield school match in 2013, captained India to the 2018 under-19 World Cup, and in October 2018 scored a century on his Test debut, the youngest Indian to do so. He played five Tests, six one-day internationals and one T20 international; he was suspended for a doping violation in 2019, scored 379 for Mumbai against Assam in the 2022–23 Ranji Trophy, moved to Maharashtra for domestic cricket from 2025–26, and is listed with the Delhi Capitals in 2026. [n27; Wikipedia: Prithvi Shaw] Ayush Mhatre, born in 2007, debuted for Mumbai at seventeen in the 2024–25 Irani Cup, scored 176 in the Ranji Trophy against Maharashtra, became the youngest player to score 150 or more in men’s List A cricket, joined Chennai Super Kings as a replacement in 2025 and scored 94 off 48 balls against Royal Challengers Bengaluru, and in 2026 captained India to its sixth under-19 World Cup, scoring 214 runs in seven matches. Vasai-Virar held a rally for him. [n27; Wikipedia: Ayush Mhatre; FPJ] A search summary says he was named Mumbai’s vice-captain for the 2026–27 Ranji opener. [n27] Shardul Thakur, born in Palghar in 1991, who played in India’s 2023 World Cup runner-up squad, belongs to the wider district, not to the city; his Wikipedia page mentions no Vasai link. [n27]

Other champions appear more briefly. Sagar Shelar of Nallasopara, described as an international athlete and Asian gold medallist, was appointed coach of the Indian mixed boxing team and welcomed across the city; the notes read only the headline. [n27; FPJ] Harshvardhan Joshi’s Everest climb of 2021 has already been told.

The dance capital

The most surprising cultural story of the period comes from Vasai’s dance floors. Suresh Mukund, a choreographer, co-founded a crew called Fictitious with Vernon Monteiro in 2009; renamed the SNV Group in 2010, it won the third season of India’s Got Talent in 2011. Mukund then founded Kings United India. His crews won Boogie Woogie in 2009, took bronze at the World Hip Hop Dance Championship in 2015, the first Indian medal there, and in 2019 won the third season of NBC’s World of Dance in the United States and its prize of a million dollars. He was nominated for an Emmy for choreography, and choreographed the film Street Dancer 3D (2020); the film ABCD 2 was reportedly inspired by Fictitious. In January 2016 he opened an academy, the Kingdom of Art, in Vasai. [n27; Wikipedia: Kings United; The Week, 25 May 2019; Udayavani] Shubham Verma founded the Tuttix Crew in Virar, mixing tutting and robotics. A 2019 Free Press Journal feature called its story “the moving tale of how Kings United turned Vasai-Virar into India’s dance capital”. [n27; FPJ, 2019]

The fort on screen

Vasai Fort, the ruined Portuguese city of the Province of the North, became one of the most filmed ruins in India. Films and songs shot there include Josh, Khamoshi: The Musical, Aag, Bhoomi, Munna Michael, Thackeray, Zero, Love Ke Liye Kuch Bhi Karega, “Pyaar Tune Kya Kiya” from Kambakkht Ishq, the Kurbaan title song and “Poster Lagwa Do” from Luka Chuppi. Major Lazer and DJ Snake shot “Lean On” in Vasai in March 2015, and Coldplay shot part of “Hymn for the Weekend”, released on 29 January 2016, at Bassein Fort with a Holi theme. [n13; Wikipedia: Vasai; vasai.com] Police booked a production company for lighting a stove inside the fort against Archaeological Survey rules. [n13; FPJ] The ruins where Gonsalo Garcia went to school, where the Captain of the North kept his court, and which Chimaji Appa’s army took in 1739, now appear in the backgrounds of music videos watched by hundreds of millions.

The festivals of December

The two civic rituals continued. The thirty-sixth Kala-Krida Mahotsav ran eighty-five competitions with more than sixty thousand contestants. [n13; FPJ] In October 2026 Sakal reported that preparations for the thirty-seventh were picking up pace under its working president Prakash Vanmali, general secretary Keval Vartak and organising secretary Anil Waz, with entry forms going to schools before Diwali and demonstrations of squash and pickleball planned. Hitendra Thakur is not mentioned. [n20; eSakal, 18 Oct 2026] The thirteenth Mayor’s Marathon was scheduled for 13 December 2026, with registration from 15 October, run by the corporation and the Vasai Taluka Kala-Krida Vikas Mandal. [n29; FPJ; UNI] A Navbharat headline, “Vasai Virar marathon cancelled due to BJP-BVA clash”, exists in the notes without a year or an opened article, and cannot be placed. [n29, Gaps]

The music of the old communities survived at the edges. East Indian brass bands, “an integral part” of village and wedding life, were showcased at Mumbai’s first East Indian Battle of the Bands. [n13; FPJ] In 2018 a group called Ghumat Gang played at a Vadval East Indian wedding in Vasai. [n27; East Indian Memory Co.] The East Indian Christmas of Vasai, described in December 2025, still meant roast chicken stuffed with offal and croutons and seasoned with bottle masala, fugias, pork indyal, sorpotel made with sugarcane vinegar, guava cheese and spiced wine; “where Goan recipes tend to favour wet masalas,” the chef Freny Fernandes said, “East Indian dishes rely on the dry bottle masala”. [n07; Slurrp, 24 Dec 2025]

Languages in 2026

What did the coast speak in 2026? No census had been taken since 2011, so the honest answer begins with the 2011 figures: a city in which Marathi was the first language of 46.49 per cent, Hindi of 26.58, Gujarati of 9.26, Bhojpuri of 3.10, Urdu of 2.72 and Marwari of 1.78. [n22] Fifteen years of migration later, those shares have certainly shifted, but by how much no source says. The notes found no source at all on the politics of language in the city, signboard rules or Marathi-Hindi disputes, because the Marathi papers that would carry them could not be searched. [n22, Gaps]

Beneath the big languages, the old dialects of the coast were spoken mostly by the old. Kadodi, or Samvedi, the speech of the Samvedi Brahmins, the Pachkalshis and the Samvedi Christians, was put at about 50,000 speakers in sixteen villages of north Vasai, from Agashi to Bhuigaon, by Saamana in March 2026, and at about 60,000 by Ethnologue. [n20; n22; Saamana, 22 Mar 2026; Wikipedia: Kadodi] Saamana described its speakers as “a singing community”, their speech sing-song in intonation, rich in folk songs, proverbs and stories. [n20] It had found its scholars: Dr Naresh Naik of Virar, whose Samvedi Boli: Sanrachana ani Swarup (“Samvedi dialect: structure and form”) won the P. N. G. Kalelkar award in 2018 and the Krishna Mukund Ujalambkar Smriti award; and Paul Rumav of Nandakhal, editor of its folk songs and proverbs. [n20; mr.wikipedia] In 2024 it entered the age of machines: a paper at an international workshop on Asian translation presented a Kadodi dataset of 949 dictionary entries and 942 sentence triples produced by native speakers, claiming it as the first machine-translation work involving the dialect. Its sample sentence was tou majuri karon pout bhartae, “he makes a living by working as a labourer”. [n22; Kumar et al., WAT 2024]

The East Indian Marathi of the Christian villages, which the Free Press Journal in 2024 called “declining, if not in danger of extinction”, was being gathered into a dictionary under the Mobai Gaothan Panchayat, ideated by Gleason Barretto and edited by Amarylisa Gonsalves, with a second edition targeted for the end of 2024. [n13; n34; FPJ, 14 Mar 2024] Fr Victor Dalmet, a priest from Uttan, composed songs in the dialect and was named “East Indian Priest of the Year”. [n34; FPJ, 30 Nov 2023] The singer Royston D’Silva wrote songs in it, though his Vasai tie is unconfirmed. [n13; n27] Vadvali, as above, was “threatened”. [n41] Warli, the largest Adivasi language of the district, had about 390,000 speakers in the 2011 census, and a Palghar teacher was profiled teaching in it. [n22; PARI] Katkari, Thakar, Kokna, Agri and Mangeli were not counted at all. [n22, Gaps]

A city of two million, then, in which the commuter on the Virar fast might speak Hindi or Bhojpuri, the shopkeeper Gujarati or Marwari, the councillor Marathi, the grandmother in Gass Kadodi, and the grandmother in Manikpur the East Indian Marathi with its kadair and zanel. The coast that had once heard Prakrit, Persian, Arabic, Gujarati and Portuguese on its docks was again a place of many tongues, though the newest ones came by train.

The last months: 2026

The smaller news of the final months, as the sources record it, fills in the picture of an ordinary year in a large Indian city.

On 18 April 2026 a fifteen-year-old from Mumbai died at a water-park resort at Arnala, according to a local site. [n06; vasaivirar.org] In September 2026 there was a cross-party agitation in Vasai-Virar against smart electricity meters and higher bills, reported by a Hindi local outlet. [n06; Metro City Samachar] In the same month a construction worker fell to his death from the fifth floor of a site at Bolinj, in Virar West, reported as the fourth fatal construction accident in about a month. [n29; Urban Acres] Bolinj was one of the garden villages of the Sopara agar in 1882; in 2026 it was a building site. [n31] In November 2026 five people died in twenty-four hours on the railway between Vasai and Vaitarna. [n29; Aapla Mahanagar] The corporation’s Virar-Alibaug corridor waited for funding; the Uttan-Virar sea link waited for its project report; the Vadhvan port signed its agreements. [n06] And on 15 November the water warning came. [n06; eSakal]

Where Sopara and Vasai stand, November 2026

Stand, at the end, where this book has so often stood: at Sopara.

The stupa mound that Bhagwanlal Indraji opened in April 1882, the Burud Rajacha Kot, is protected and cared for. Its relics, the stone coffer, the nested caskets of copper, silver, stone, crystal and gold, the gold flowers and the fragments of a begging bowl believed by tradition to have been used by the Buddha, are in the Asiatic Society of Mumbai; the fragment of Ashoka’s eighth rock edict that Indraji found near the Bhatela pond, and the fragment of the ninth found at Bhuigaon on 1 January 1956 (by N. A. Gore, in Sircar’s account; by a little boy, in a later paper), are in the museum once called the Prince of Wales. [n02; Asiatic Society of Mumbai; Sircar, EI 32] Around the mound stands a town of more than half a million people. Of the ancient port, “no traces remain”, only a few protected mounds and a dried-up lake. [n02; LiveHistoryIndia] The Bhatela pond still holds the remains of a Portuguese jetty and customs house. [n31] A jogging track rings the Chakreshwar lake. [n10] The old village of Sopara, with its mohallas of Nawayat, Kharkhandi and Bazar Peth, lives on as Sopara Gaon, a locality of Nallasopara West. [n31] The name Shurparak Nagar, which Wikipedia calls the city’s official name, has no source behind it. [n31]

Stand at Vasai. The fort of São Sebastião, the Portuguese city where the General of the North kept his court and where four orders kept six churches, four convents and two colleges, is a ruin maintained by the Archaeological Survey, a film set, and once a year, on the Sunday of the neap tide, a church again. [n38; n09; n13] Across the island, the cathedral at Papdi, restored by its own parishioners, carries a UNESCO award. [n11] The diocese has its third bishop born on the island. The saint born at Agashi is honoured at Gass, and his church there was desecrated three years ago and still stands. [n38] The village of Umralla, a Kupari village of the garden belt, is a pilgrim centre for the Year of St Francis. [n38]

Look at the land. The western island is still green, because a Franciscan and a hundred thousand people made the state change a plan, and because, as his colleague said at his funeral, the movement held. The flower farmers of Arnala still send mogra and sonchafa to Dadar, fewer flowers in hotter, wetter years, without insurance. [n17] The ponds that survive are being restored by volunteers, and where they are, the wells sweeten. [n17] East of the railway, the salt waste of the Gazetteer is a city of towers on fill, and it floods every monsoon; the railway, the activists and the scientists have all said why, and the report that told the state what to do in 2018 has not been carried out. [n06; n17]

Look at the sea. The Kolis of Arnala, Pachubandar and Naigaon still go out, for pomfret mostly, against trawlers and purse-seiners and LED lights, and they have shut their harbours in protest against a port fifty kilometres to the north whose first phase is due in 2029. [n17; n20; n06] The mangroves of the creek are cut and defended, cut and defended. [n17]

Look at the people. A corporation of well over a million in 2011, probably above two million now, though no census has counted them. [n06] Less than half of them spoke Marathi as a first language in 2011. [n22] The Kuparis and the Vadvals, the gardeners who built the wadis, are uncounted minorities in their own villages, keeping their identities in associations, dictionaries, websites, festivals and feasts, their dialects listed as threatened. [n34; n41] The Thakurs lost the legislature in 2024 and kept the corporation in 2026; the BVA’s mayor sits with a Catholic deputy; the BJP sits opposite with forty-three seats. [n06] The man accused for thirty-four years of a murder on the Nallasopara platform is acquitted. The commissioner arrested for the forty-one buildings was released by a High Court that called the case against him speculative. The families of Agarwal Nagari have no homes there. The families of Ramabai Apartment have five lakh rupees each. [n06; n26; n29]

And look at the train. Fifteen-coach locals run north from Virar to Dahanu. Two new lines are a third built from Borivali to Virar, due in 2029. [n06; n29] Every morning the Virar fast pulls out full, as the four o’clock train once pulled out with Vasai’s bananas in baskets on shoulder poles. The coast still sends its produce south. Only now the produce is people.

Shurparaka was a port of the western sea, whose ships, the Jataka tales say, traded with south-west Asia, Gujarat, Malabar and Sri Lanka, and where, the Buddhist texts say, a merchant named Purna built a sandalwood temple and invited the Buddha. Baçaim was the Court of the North, a city of churches inside walls. Vasai-Virar is a city on the edge of a megacity, a dormitory and a corridor, with a ruin at one end and a port being built beyond the other. The island of Sopara, which the Gazetteer’s writer saw cut off by a silting creek in 1882, is now joined to everything. What it holds of its own, in its gardens and dialects and feasts and ponds, is what the rest of this book, in the chapters on the Kuparis and the threads that run through every era, goes back to find.
