What was spoken and written on the coast of Sopara and Vasai, by whom, then and now, with sample words. Built from notes 22, 13, 20, 34, 35 and 41.

How to use this appendix

  • Primary text: Grierson’s Linguistic Survey of India vol. VII (1905), the Gazetteers (1882, 1909), Enthoven (1920-22), Sircar (Epigraphia Indica 32). Secondary: Wikipedia, a 2024 Kadodi paper, community blogs. Each row says which.
  • Spelling of Marathi words is as in the notes (transliteration or Devanagari). Grierson’s OCR is damaged; his glosses are approximate and one (Vadval hat) is corrected in §3.5.
  • Citations [nNN; source]. No language in this appendix has a complete modern grammar; the gaps are in App. G §5.

3. Languages in layers (note 22)

3.1 Earliest written layers

Language / script Evidence at or near Sopara Notes and conflicts Cite
Pre-Indo-Aryan substrate None specific to this coast found Grierson links Koli to “Munda tribes (Kols)” only as a past opinion (“have often been connected”): weak [n22]
Prakrit in Brahmi (Ashokan) Rock Edict VIII (1882, near Bhatela pond, about one third of the edict) and Rock Edict IX (1 Jan 1956, Bhuigaon, a little over half); script Brahmi, language Prakrit; line-1 words Devanampiye Piyadasi, line 4 amnaye chu edisaye; letter forms: cork-screw “r”, plough and tripartite “y” (Sircar, EI 32). Content (from the Kalsi version): RE VIII contrasts earlier kings’ hunting “pleasure-tours” with Ashoka’s “tours of morality” (after his tenth year, visit to Sambodhi); RE IX says ceremonies at illness, marriage, birth and journeys bear little fruit while morality (courtesy to servants, reverence to elders, gentleness to animals, gifts to ascetics and Brahmanas) bears much. Findspot conflict: one Wikipedia version puts both in an old Muslim graveyard near Ramkund and the 1956 fragment as RE XI; prefer Sircar. Hultzsch 1925 transcription not pulled. [n22; n02]
Prakrit donor records Kanheri: cistern given by the merchant (negama) Samika/Svamika “from Soparaga (Surparaka)”; a second Kanheri record: a jeweller and a merchant from Sopara; Cave 21 (Gautamiputra Satakarni) is by a Kalyan family; Nasik: Rishabhadatta/Ushavadata, son-in-law of Nahapana, garbled “Sorparaga” (identification is the search tool’s reading); Naneghat Brahmi (2nd-1st c. BCE) on the route Sopara-Kalyan-Thana to Nasik, Paithan, Ter, no mention of Sopara confirm readings with Luders [n22]
Sanskrit (Shilahara era) Land grants (e.g. AD 1150 Haripaldev grant naming Vatteraka); copper-plate text not read [n22; n40]
Old Marathi Akshi (Raigad) stone inscription under Keshideva: grain gift to goddess Danyadevi, Sanskrit and Marathi in Devanagari; date c. 1012 CE (Tulpule Shaka 934) vs Shaka 1132 (Dikshit); competing “earliest Marathi”: 739 CE Satara plate, Dive plate (1060 or 1086) “earliest” depends on definition [n22]
Modi script Administrative script for Marathi, 17th to mid-19th century; Balbodh Devanagari became primary; official use to 1952 by one account; Peshwa records largely in Modi (about four crore pages); letters of Bajirao and Chimaji Appa on Commons (readings unverified) [n22]
Persian / Arabic Under the Gujarat Sultanate: no source found [n22]
Portuguese and the Norteiro creole “Norteiro” = Indo-Portuguese varieties of the Northern Province (Daman to Chaul/Korlai, including Bassein and Bombay); described by Dalgado (1906); survivors: Korlai (about 760 speakers), Daman (about 4,000), Diu (about 180), Cannanore (5; Cochin creole’s last speaker had died). Grierson 1905: Thana Roman Catholics (Kinstav, about 26,600) had adopted Portuguese words but speak “nothing else than the usual Marathi of the district”: no creole by 1900-05. The Kadodi paper calls its Indo-Portuguese ingredient “(now extinct)”. no Bassein-specific creole structure found [n22; Cardoso 2006; Open Magazine]
English, Hindi/Urdu, Gujarati Konkani Musalmans’ dialect has “a considerable amount of Hindostani words”; in north Thana Marathi “gradually superseded by Gujarati” (Grierson) [n22]

3.2 Living dialects (Grierson 1905 and modern)

Dialect Speakers then and now Features Sample words Cite
Samvedi / Kadodi (ISO smv) Grierson: about 2,700 (Bassein and Alibag; “Samvedis live as husbandmen and gardeners”); now 50,000 (Saamana, 16 villages incl. Gass) to 60,000 (Ethnologue via Wikipedia); do not present growth (different counts) “a mechanical mixture of Konkan Marathi and Gujarati”; ch/chh become s/h; s becomes h; masculine nominatives in -o; dative -do; past 3rd masc. -o. Modern: lacks ऐ, औ and च, छ, ण, ष; श and स often become ह; plain dentals preferred; omits present “is” (ahe, Kadodi hai); “a mix of Konkani, Gujarati, Marathi and Indo-Portuguese (now extinct)”; oral; 949 dictionary entries and 942 sentence triples (2024 WAT dataset) haen cow dung (Marathi shen); tou majuri karon pout bhartae “He makes a living by working as a laborer”; tou nejlay “He is sleeping” (Marathi to zhopla ahe); Grierson: hakra “son”, hit “field”, genitive -ho/-ha; mansa-do “to the man”; kadodi.in magazine [n22]
Vadvali / Phudagi Grierson: Vadval a sub-tribe of “Prankali/Panchkalshi” Kunbis, 3,500 in Thana (Bassein, Mahim, Dahanu), “supposed to have immigrated from Gujarat”; Phudagi 1,000 speakers, “dialect of a wandering tribe”, “almost identical with Vadval”; Wikipedia: Vadvali = Phudagi, Panchkalshi and Chaukalshi (weak) “gh” becomes s/h; s and sh become h (“like ch in loch”); cerebral l as in the Deccan; Gujarati words: ne chha, mere “near”, bhuke “with hunger”; Specimen 36 hona “gold”, hat “sit/seven”, sakar “servant”, pani/pant “water”, sand “moon”; Eka don por hote “One man-to two sons were”; kapnaylya-var “after having spent”; ghan dukal “mighty famine”; no modern word list [n22]
East Indian / Kinstav (Christian Marathi) Grierson: about 26,600 in Thana (“Kinstav” to neighbours; “Portuguese or Salsette Christians” to Europeans) usual Marathi with Portuguese words pay “father”, may “mother”, mass, padron “god-father”, madron “god-mother”, estate (English); Specimen 14; “old Puran by Vas de Guimaraens” Marathi text [n22; n13]
Koli 163,000 in Thana (189,186 across Bombay, Thana, Kolaba, Janjira) “everywhere speak the dialect of their neighbours” [n22]
Kunbi / Agri Kunbi 350,000 in Thana (368,000 with Janjira); Agri salt-makers and rice farmers, own dialect in Kolaba (22,826); no Thana count [n22]
Mangela 11,538 (1901 census); fishermen of Mahim, Bassein, Dahanu “of the same kind as Vadval, Samvedi, with a strong admixture of Gujarati”; Specimen 38 [n22]
Varli (Warli) Grierson 89,000 in Thana, over half of Dahanu; about 390,000 (2011, Wikipedia; 387,481 archived); UNESCO “potentially vulnerable” originally a Bhil dialect, Marathi-influenced; Gujarati form in the extreme north nang-ne “to see”; vichai for vichaila “he asked” [n22]
Katkari (Kathodi) count table damaged Bhil dialect related to Khandeshi, Marathi-influenced; name from kath (catechu/khair) [n22]
Thakar (Thakur) hill country of south-east Thana between Konkan and Deccan with Gujarati admixture; agent case in -han mahan “by me”, tuhan “by thee” [n22]
Konkan Standard about 2.5 million, Daman to Rajapur; Parabhi/Damani (Prabhu/Kayastha) 16,000 in Thana [n22]
Kokna not found at all [n22]
  • Grierson’s “three small dialects in Thana (Vadval, Phudgi, Samvedi) which still have preserved many of the characteristic features of Gujarati Bhili”; Konkani Musalmans “chiefly of Salsette, Kalyan and Bhiwandi”. The same s-to-h change appears in Grierson (1905) and the 2024 Kadodi paper: 120 years continuous. [n22]
  • Census 2011 mother tongues: city: Marathi 46.49%, Hindi 26.58%, Gujarati 9.26%, Bhojpuri 3.10%, Urdu 2.72%, Marwari 1.78%; Vasai taluka: Marathi 49.97%, Hindi 24.78%, Gujarati 8.60%; religion: Hindu 78.05%, Muslim 8.45%, Christian 8.25%, Buddhist 2.53%, Jain 1.75%. Virar page (conflicting): Gujarati 28.30%, Hindi 15.31%. Nallasopara 2001: Hindi 22.92%, Gujarati 17.82%, Urdu 12% minority speakers. [n22; n31]
  • Place-name etymologies: Sopara < Shurparaka (loosely “city of braves”; “Shurpa = winnowing basket” unverified); Nala/Nalla none; Vasai < Sanskrit vas “residence” (alt. basti, Jain settlement: speculative); Virar < Eka-Veera (folk); Arnala = Ilha das Vacas; Agashi, Nirmal (Vimal or nir+mal “sea hill”), Papdi, Naigaon, Gass none. [n22]
  • The sound ळ: present in Marathi, Gujarati, Konkani, Odia, Rajasthani; Dravidian contact commonly cited but possibly Vedic; example dadima > dalimba; present in Vasai speech 1905 (Vadval “cerebral l”). [n22]

  • Kadodi/Samvedi (n13): ISO smv; intermediate Marathi-Konkani dialect with Portuguese lexical and phonetic influence and Goan features; about 60,000 speakers (Ethnologue via Wikipedia, 2016); traditionally oral, written in Devanagari, no literary tradition. Vadvali and Samvedi have higher shares of Portuguese-derived words than ordinary Marathi; sources disagree (“very few” vs a mix of Gujarati, Marathi and Indo-Portuguese). Wikipedia’s Vasai languages: Marathi (official), Samvedi, Agri, Vadavli, Konkani, Koli. South Vasai (Vadval) speech and dress differ slightly from North Vasai (Kupari) (Vasaiker). Endangerment: “declining, if not in danger of extinction”, mostly senior speakers; East Indian Dictionary under the Mobai Gaothan Panchayat (Gleason Barretto, ideator and founder editor; Amarylisa Gonsalves editing; second revised edition targeted end 2024); singer Royston D’Silva writes songs in the dialect. [n13; FPJ 2024]

3.4 Samvedi / Kadodi in Marathi sources (note 20)

  • Saamana (22 Mar 2026, Varnika Kakde): Samvedi (Kadodi, कादोडी) “spoken in north Vasai since about the 6th century” (unsupported claim); speakers Samvedi Brahmins, Pachkalshe and Samvedi Christians, about 50,000; mixes Marathi, Konkani and Portuguese with some Gujarati; sing-song intonation (“a singing community”); folk songs, proverbs, stories; twelve coastal villages “from Agashi to Bhuigaon”. [n20; Saamana]
  • The 16 Samvedi villages (Saamana and mr.wikipedia): Bhuigaon, Wagholi, Kofrad, Gas (Gass), Umrale, Bolinj, Gavdhani, Vatar, Valunje, Vanda (Mardes), Padhai, Karmale, Navale, Somadi, Bankar, Mandpe. [n20]
  • Scholarship: Paul Rumav (Nandakhal teacher) edited Samvedi Lokgeete, Madhachya Ghagri (Pots of honey), Samvedi Khristi Samaj, Vasai Parisaratil Mhani (proverbs); Dr Naresh Naik (Virar) wrote Samvedi Boli: Sanrachana ani Swarup (Krishna Mukund Ujalambkar Smriti award; P. Na. Go. Kalelkar award 2018); the Kadodi journal. [n20]
  • Sound features and sample words: “alo” (came) > “alo” with open o (आलॉ); “jevlo” (ate) > “jivlo” (जिवलॉ); initial s to h: sukeli > hukeli (हुकेळी), sheni > heni (हेनी); “jevlat ka?” (“have you eaten?”) answered “jivyasa hay” (“I’ve eaten”). [n20; mr.wikipedia, partly tagged “needs citation”]
  • Vadvali = Phudagi (Panchkalshi and Chaukalshi; English Wikipedia, weak). Agri: Marathi Wikipedia groups Kupari and Vadval with Agri, Gavli, Kunbi, Karadi, Kumbhar (farming bhumiputra castes) and the seafaring Koli, Kharvi, Bhoi, Bhandari in the wider “Agri samaj” of the coastal agar belt; Vasai-Virar speech somewhat nasal (sanunasik). Christian Kolis: Portuguese of Vasai converted Hindu Kolis (mr.wikipedia). Vadvali sample words: none found. [n20]
  • Gaps: Kupari and Vadval houses, jewellery (the lugade), wedding songs, revival groups: no Marathi web source. [n20]

3.5 Corrections and additions from notes 34, 35 and 41

  • Grierson’s “Alabim” = Mahim (correction to n22): the Gazetteer (XIII p.82) says Samvedis are found “only in Bassein and Mahim”, so Grierson’s OCR “Alabim” is probably Mahim, not Alibag. Samvedi number 2,700 (Hindu); Christians counted separately as Kinstav/Kiristav. [n34]
  • Kinstav, 1882: “Their home tongue is Marathi, very little different from the Marathi spoken by the Kunbis and Kolis of the district. A few know and a considerable number understand an ungrammatical Portuguese, and among some of the higher families and in the Khatri ward of Thana town Portuguese is the home speech. Latin is the language of the Church.” Sermons in Portuguese or Marathi; prayer books in both (Gaz. XIII pp.204, 207). Christa Purana (reported 1659 per Gazetteer; elsewhere Kristapurana 1616 by Thomas Stephens SJ, 10,962 verses; Devanagari edition in East Indian dialect printed at Vasai 1922, reprinted 1927; Nelson Falcao 2009 dual edition) “still well known and much read” in the dialect of the Thana Son Kolis. Mendiratta p.241: in the 1880s Portuguese was a home language only for well-to-do families in Bandra, Thane, Bassein, Kurla; “Indo-Portuguese creoles were more widespread in Salsette and Bassein and survived until later”; majority of uneducated East Indians used Marathi or the creole; Daman bishops’ Portuguese schools (1887-1925) “don’t appear to have been long-lasting”. Portuguese loans in the Christian dialect are church and kinship terms (entered through the parish). [n34; n38]
  • Kupari life-cycle terms (D’Mello blog; unverified): Baltim baptism; Komsar confession; Cominyao First Communion; Chrisma confirmation; Hamdao, Lagin, Varad wedding stages; Saudio post-wedding celebration; Yentar funeral; Saatvoh seventh-day Mass and meal; Paya bharni foundation ceremony (jewellery buried, coconut broken); Bejimat housewarming blessing; Pirsao / miravnuk Lenten candle procession; Upas 40-day fast. Surname Rumao, Rumao Ali. [n07]
  • Vadvali / Phudagi detail (n41, correcting n22): Grierson’s sound law is ch > s/h (not gh > s): sakar (Marathi chakar) “servant”, sand (chand) “moon”, sar (char) “four”; s/sh > h: hona “gold”, hat “field” (n22 glossed “sit/seven”), hambhar “hundred”; genitive -sa becomes -ha: babaha, babaga, babaa “of the father”; initial s dropped in ay/hay “is”; cerebral l as in the Deccan (dola eye, mil-la was found); n often dental (pani water); Gujarati: mere “near”, potali “wife”, bhuke “with hunger”, lyat-na “from among them”; agent case in -n (babān “by the father”); genitives mād, mādzā, tād, tudzā; verb hāy/āy; past geltāv; perfect ālāy “he has come”. Specimen 36: eka don por hote “one man two sons were”; mag tyan dhak’la dzak’la paisa udyaya lag’la “then the younger son began to squander all the money”. Phudgi list: ghoda horse, pani/pant water, sakar servant, sar four, hona gold, sat seven, visa twenty. PLSI 17.2 (Smita Patil, “Wadavali”, 2017, relayed): sakhar “sugar” > hakar, sanj “evening” > hanj, shimpi “tailor” > himpi, chamcha “spoon” > samcha, chinch “tamarind” > shis, bhint “wall” > bhitya, pahat “dawn” > pat, aishi “eighty” > arshi. Bombay City Gazetteer 1909: Panchkalshi true language “closely akin to that of the Prabhus and resembles the Marathi of the Dnyaneshwari” with Gujarati words; lower-class women’s home dialect “hardly distinguishable from a Koli fish-wife”, who says kain “where”, mijim “by me”, gehlum “I had gone”. 1882 Gazetteer: “incorrect Marathi using l for ḷ and n for ṇ” (conflicts with Grierson). Glottolog phud1238 / ISO phd: “threatened”; about 2,500 speakers (Ethnologue 2000, Wikipedia: Dahanu, Naigaon, Thane); 1931 Census lists Vadval under Marathi. Vadval Samaj site post “The Inner Workings of the Vadvali Language” (content not shown). Edwardes: home tongue of Prabhus, Panchkalshis and Bhandaris holds many words from the Koli and Agri. [n41]
  • Other community speech (n35): Prabhus “incorrect Marathi” with Gujarati furniture words (dadar ladder, takabari wall bookcase, ovara room, ota veranda); Son Koli “a patois of Marathi gradually obsolescent under the grinding of the Local Board school and the newspaper” (Enthoven 1920), tavade for tikde “there”, avade for ikade “here”; Mangela, Machhi, Kharvi, Kharpatil “speak Gujarati at home”; Katkari “a slightly disguised Marathi with many Gujarati and Hindustani words”; Thakur “Marathi spoken with a long drawl”; Varli words nange “see”, tal “here”, davar “old man”, jhinguor “blanket”, yayki/tup “ghee”, clipping kotja for kolhe jatos; Konkani Muslims: “Konkani Marathi with a slightly peculiar pronunciation and a mixture of Urdu, Persian and Arab words”; Nawayathi (Canara) Konkani with Arabic, Persian, Urdu; Bene Israel Marathi with r for l; Parsis adopted Gujarati (Kisseh-i-Sanjan condition: give up the language of Persia); Lad Vanis speak Gujarati among themselves and Marathi with others; Masudi (AD 915): the “Lar language” spoken in coast towns as far south as Chaul. Wider pattern: immigrant groups kept a sacred liturgical tongue and shifted domestic speech to local Indo-Aryan. Gujarati superseding Marathi in the extreme north (Grierson; Gaz.). Modern migrant languages: Hindi, Bhojpuri, Urdu, Marwari; Vasai taluka 2011 Bhojpuri 2.92%, Urdu 2.52%, Marwari 1.64%. [n35]
  • Gaps: no linguistic study of Kadodi’s formation; origin of the name “Kadodi” unknown; Specimen 37 (Samvedi) unreadable in OCR; no modern Vadvali fieldwork. [n34; n41]
